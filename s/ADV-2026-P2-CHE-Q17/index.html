What it teaches

  • The stem prints one molar mass, 350 for K, and that number is a checksum rather than decoration: whatever structure is proposed for K has to weigh exactly 350 on the given atomic masses, which confirms the whole sequence before any arithmetic begins
  • m-Xylene's two methyl groups are both o,p-directing, so the acyl group enters the one ring position activated by both of them
  • The chloride is exchanged for iodide before the phenoxide step because iodide is the better leaving group - the Finkelstein exchange is there to serve the substitution that follows
  • Sodium borohydride is chosen for its selectivity: it reduces the ketone to a secondary alcohol and leaves the aromatic nitro group completely untouched, which is exactly why the nitro group survives all the way to L
  • L contains TWO nitrogen atoms - one amine and one nitro - and the entire question turns on that fact being noticed
  • Kjeldahl's digestion converts nitrogen to ammonium only from amine-like nitrogen; nitro, azo, diazo and ring nitrogen are not reduced and are never estimated, so only one of L's two nitrogens is seen
  • The method therefore under-reports a nitro compound: it finds about 4.9 per cent nitrogen in L where 9.8 per cent is actually present, and the gap is the nitro nitrogen
  • Sulphuric acid is dibasic: 2 NH3 + H2SO4 gives (NH4)2SO4, so the moles of acid are half the moles of ammonia - a factor that is trivial to state and easy to drop
