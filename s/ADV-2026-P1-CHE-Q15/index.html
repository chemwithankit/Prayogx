Ozonolysis and intramolecular aldol of bicyclic ring-fusion alkenes: matching four alkenes to their beta-hydroxy ketones

Four bicyclic alkenes whose only C=C is the ring-fusion bond, watched at two levels at once. On the left a flask at −78 °C takes ozone until it turns blue, then zinc dust and water, then aqueous NaOH on a hot plate; on the right every electron pair moves as a curved arrow: 1,3-dipolar cycloaddition, retro-cycloaddition, recombination to the secondary ozonide, two-step zinc reduction to the cyclodecanedione, the more stable enolate and the intramolecular aldol that closes two new rings, then protonation. What it teaches

  • The ring-fusion C=C is cut: a 6-6 alkene gives cyclodecane-1,6-dione, a 7-5 alkene cyclodecane-1,5-dione
  • Zn / H2O reduces the ozonide to two ketones (and ZnO) without further oxidation
  • The more stable enolate forms at the CH that bears CH3 (more substituted C=C), provided the ring it closes has at least five atoms
  • The enolate attacks the other C=O across the ring: 6-6 alkenes give 5-7 fused beta-hydroxy ketones, 7-5 alkenes give decalins
  • P → 2, Q → 1, R → 5, S → 3: option C
