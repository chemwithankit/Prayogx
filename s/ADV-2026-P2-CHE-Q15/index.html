What it teaches

  • Molality counts moles of solute per kilogram of solvent while mole fraction counts them against every mole present, so a molality always has to be converted before Raoult's law can be used - and the conversion is what the molar mass of A is given for
  • The mass of solvent taken is a free choice: x(B) = m / (m + 1000/M(A)) contains no w(A), so the answer is the same for 500 g or 2500 g of A
  • For an ideal solution each component's partial pressure is its pure vapour pressure times its own mole fraction, and the total is a straight line between p(A) and p(B)
  • With p(total), p(A) and both mole fractions known, Raoult's law has exactly one unknown left, which is what makes the first half of this question a one-line solve
  • A liquid's molar volume comes from density alone - M/rho - with no temperature and no pressure in it, because its molecules are already touching
  • Molar volume of pure B in the vapour phase means one mole of B alone at its own vapour pressure p(B), not at the mixture's total pressure and not at 1 atm - this is the single decision the whole question turns on
  • R quoted in L atm K-1 mol-1 forces the pressure into atmospheres, which is why the stem also hands over 1 atm = 760 mm Hg
  • Both molar volumes are per mole, so the ratio is a pure number: RTrho x 1000 / (M x p)
