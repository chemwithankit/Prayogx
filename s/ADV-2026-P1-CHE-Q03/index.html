What it teaches

  • A bond between atoms of different electronegativity is polar; its dipole points (chemists' crossed arrow) from the delta-plus to the delta-minus atom
  • A lone pair on the central atom puts extra negative charge on one side of it and carries a moment of its own, pointing from the atom out into the pair
  • The dipole moment of a molecule is the vector sum of its bond and lone-pair moments, so the shape decides whether they add or cancel
  • BF3 is trigonal planar (3 bond pairs, no lone pair): three equal B-F moments at 120 degrees sum to exactly zero even though each bond is strongly polar
  • NH4+ is tetrahedral (4 bond pairs, no lone pair): four equal N-H moments to the corners of a tetrahedron sum to zero; the ion's charge is symmetric about N so it has no dipole about its centre
  • In a trigonal pyramid the sideways parts of the three bond moments cancel and 3 mu_b cos(beta) survives along the axis, with cos^2(beta) = (1 + 2 cos alpha)/3
  • In NH3 the N-H moments point toward N, the same way as the lone pair, so they add: 1.47 D measured
  • In NF3 the N-F moments point toward F, away from the lone pair, so they nearly cancel: 0.23 D measured, although N-F is the more polar bond
