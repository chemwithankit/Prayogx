Collinear carbons in X: double acetylide alkylation of buta-1,3-diyne with trans-crotyl bromide

JEE Advanced 2026 Paper 1 · Question 12 Chemistry Alkynes - acidity of terminal alkynes, acetylide SN2 alkylation and linear sp geometry Easy-Moderate

Buta-1,3-diyne, two equivalents of NaNH2 and excess trans-crotyl bromide, watched at two levels at once: the liquid-ammonia flask on the left, the molecules on the right, in step. Curved arrows move each electron pair as NH2- pulls off both acidic sp C-H protons and each acetylide carbanion attacks a CH2-Br from the back (SN2, Walden umbrella flip), while the grey NaNH2 is used up and white NaBr settles. X is then relaxed to its real angles (sp 180, sp2 120, sp3 109.5 degrees) and spun about its axis: the carbons that never move are counted. Products come from pushing the arrows on molecule graphs; the count is made twice, by rule and on a geometric model. No prediction stage - input, run, watch, result.

What it teaches

  • Two equivalents of NaNH2 remove both terminal sp C-H protons of buta-1,3-diyne, giving the acetylide dianion
  • Each acetylide attacks the CH2 of trans-crotyl bromide by SN2 (backside attack, one step); the trans C=C is untouched
  • X = CH3CH=CHCH2-C#C-C#C-CH2CH=CHCH3 (C12H14), with 4 sp carbons in the middle
  • An sp carbon holds its two neighbours at 180 degrees, so the four sp carbons and the two CH2 carbons bonded to them lie on one line
  • The CH2 is sp3 (109.5 degrees) and the next carbon is sp2, so the line stops there: 6
