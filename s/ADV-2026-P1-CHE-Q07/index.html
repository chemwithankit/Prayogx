What it teaches

  • PtF6 has a very high electron affinity (about 7 eV) and takes one electron from O2 (IE 12.07 eV): O2 + PtF6 -> O2+[PtF6]-, so X+ = O2+ and Y- = [PtF6]-
  • O2+ has 15 electrons; the electron removed from O2 was antibonding (pi*2p), so bonding 10 and antibonding 5 give bond order 2.5 - not 1.5, which is superoxide O2-: (A) false
  • In [PtF6]- platinum is +5; Pt [Xe]4f14 5d9 6s1 loses 6s1 and four 5d electrons, leaving 5d5 (t2g5): (B) true
  • Pt goes from +6 in PtF6 to +5 in [PtF6]-: PtF6 is reduced, so it is the oxidant: (C) true
  • All six F atoms stay on Pt and no O-F bond forms - no fluorine is transferred, so PtF6 is not acting as a fluorinating agent: (D) false
  • Bartlett noticed IE(Xe) 12.13 eV is almost the same as IE(O2) and made the first noble-gas compound the same year
