Four optical effects matched to their phenomena by measurement: the aurora's line spectrum, polarized skylight, the rainbow and diffraction fringes

JEE Advanced 2026 Paper 1 · Question 14 Physics Optical effects in nature and the phenomenon essential to each - emission, scattering, dispersion with reflection, diffraction Easy

Four stations, four measurements: the aurora shows atomic emission lines (5), skylight is polarized by molecular scattering (4), a raindrop disperses light with a partial internal reflection (1), and a slit gives dark fringes in one colour (3) - P5, Q4, R1, S3, option A.

Sub-topics covered

  • Atomic emission: lambda = hc/dE gives discrete spectral lines (aurora: O 557.7 and 630.0 nm, N2+ 427.8 nm)
  • Scattering of light by molecules: the blue sky and the partial polarization of skylight, P = sin^2 theta/(1 + cos^2 theta)
  • The rainbow: dispersion n(lambda) and one internal reflection in a raindrop; minimum deviation
  • Single-slit diffraction: dark fringes at a sin theta = m lambda
  • Total internal reflection and the critical angle

What it teaches

  • The aurora is light emitted by oxygen and nitrogen excited by charged particles from the Sun: a line spectrum, not scattered sunlight
  • Light scattered by air molecules is partially polarized, fully (ideally) at 90 deg from the Sun; total internal reflection reflects both polarizations fully and cannot polarize
  • A rainbow needs dispersion and one internal reflection; inside the drop the ray meets the back at about 40 deg, below the critical angle 48.7 deg, so the reflection is partial, not total
  • Dark and bright fringes in light of one colour behind an opening are diffraction
  • P5 Q4 R1 S3: option A
