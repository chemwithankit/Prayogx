Rate of temperature rise of a liquid heated by a radioactive nuclide produced at a constant rate

JEE Advanced 2026 Paper 2 · Question 2 Physics Radioactive nuclide produced at a constant rate - build-up to saturation and the heat its decays deliver Moderate

A reactor makes nuclide X at a constant rate alpha; its decays heat a liquid. The nuclei build up to alpha/lambda, so the heating starts at zero and levels off: dT/dt = (alpha E0/ms)(1 - e^-lambda t) - option A, found by fitting the options to the thermometer's measured rate.

Sub-topics covered

  • Radioactive decay law: activity = lambda N
  • Production at a constant rate with decay: dN/dt = alpha - lambda N
  • Saturation: N -> alpha/lambda, activity -> alpha
  • Energy released per decay heating a liquid: ms dT/dt = E0 lambda N
  • Decay heat after production stops

What it teaches

  • dN/dt = alpha - lambda N with N(0) = 0 gives N = (alpha/lambda)(1 - e^-lambda t)
  • The activity lambda N = alpha(1 - e^-lambda t) starts at zero and rises to alpha, when decay balances production
  • With no heat loss ms dT/dt = E0 lambda N, so dT/dt = (alpha E0/ms)(1 - e^-lambda t) - option A
  • Check every candidate at t = 0 (nothing has decayed, so no heating) and as t -> infinity (activity = alpha): only A passes both
  • Traps: (B) grows without limit; (C) puts lambda where the production rate belongs; (D) heats at full power before any nucleus exists
  • After the reactor stops, the nuclei present keep decaying: decay heat falls as e^-lambda t
