Sub-topics covered

  • Orbital energy of a one-electron atom: E = -13.6 Z^2/n^2 eV, depends on n only
  • Multi-electron atoms: energy depends on n and l
  • Shielding by core electrons and effective nuclear charge

  • Slater's rules
  • Radial Schroedinger equation and node counting

What it teaches

  • In hydrogen there is one electron and nothing to shield it, so the orbital energy depends only on n: E2s(H) = E2p(H) = -13.6/4 = -3.40 eV (degenerate)
  • In lithium the 2s or 2p electron moves in the field of the +3 nucleus and the 1s2 core; outside the core it feels about +1, inside up to +3
  • The 2s orbital has a small inner lobe that penetrates the 1s core; the 2p orbital has an angular node at the nucleus and stays mostly outside - so 2s feels a higher effective nuclear charge
  • Higher effective nuclear charge means lower energy: E2s(Li) < E2p(Li)
  • Zeff(Li, 2s) is greater than 1 (about 1.26 from the measured energy, 1.30 by Slater's rules), so E2s(Li) is below E2s(H): E2s(H) > E2s(Li)
  • Since E2p(H) = E2s(H) > E2s(Li), the statement E2p(H) < E2s(Li) is false
  • Order: E2s(Li) < E2p(Li) < E2s(H) = E2p(H)
