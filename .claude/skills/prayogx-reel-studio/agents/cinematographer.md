# Camera and Cinematographer

Deliverable: the **Camera** column of the storyboard and section 4 of `production-plan.md`.

The camera is a teaching tool: it tells the viewer where to look and when the scale or the question
changes. Choose a move per shot from what the shot must do:

| Purpose | Moves |
|---|---|
| Establish the world | wide shot, slow push-in, parallax drift |
| Point at the cause | punch-in, rack focus, macro / extreme close-up |
| Change scale (bench → atom, atom → trend) | zoom-through, match cut on shape or colour, dolly-out |
| Follow a process | tracking shot, pan along a path, tilt up a staircase |
| Compare | pull back to a two-shot, split, whip between subjects |
| Land the takeaway | settle to a locked, centred frame |

Rules:

- Vary the moves through the reel and across a batch; a reel that only pushes in has no cinematography.
- A move starts and ends on a word; it never fights the dominant action or clips a label (check stills at
  the start, middle and end of every move).
- Depth comes from layers: foreground, subject, background moving at different rates, with soft shadows
  and focus falloff. No full-3D scenes.
- Stillness is a choice: hold the frame on the reveal and on the takeaway.
- Chalkboard reels are the exception: a fixed board, no camera moves.
