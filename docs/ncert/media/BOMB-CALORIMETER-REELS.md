# Bomb calorimeter — vertical reels (storyboard)

Status: **storyboard for the owner's approval (2026-10-06). Nothing is produced from it yet.**
Source experience: CON-CHE-CALORIMETER-01, scene 11 (the 3-D bomb calorimeter), filmed through its explainer mode.
Replaces, for Instagram Reels / YouTube Shorts, the single 85 s landscape storyboard in
[BOMB-CALORIMETER-EXPLAINER.md](BOMB-CALORIMETER-EXPLAINER.md), whose science, assets and verification points still
apply. Format rules: owner direction 2026-10-06 — vertical 9:16, 20–60 s set by the concept, **one reel = one complete
concept** with its own beginning, explanation and takeaway; one-or-many decided here, before production.

## 1. One reel or several? — the decision

The whole idea, "how a bomb calorimeter works", runs from the apparatus to ΔU in about 220 narrated words — ≈ 85 s at a
calm teaching pace. It does not fit ~60 s without dropping a reasoning step (the calorimeter's heat capacity, or
w = 0), so **one reel is rejected**.

It contains two concepts that each stand on their own, with a natural end point:

| Concept | Starts with | Logical end point (takeaway) |
|---|---|---|
| **A. How the instrument turns a temperature rise into a heat** | the apparatus | q_V = −C ΔT |
| **B. Why that heat is ΔU** | the measured heat q_V | ΔU = q_V (constant volume → w = 0 → first law) |

A three-reel split ("what's inside", "how it measures", "why ΔU") was considered and **rejected**: "what's inside" alone
is a list of parts with no takeaway of its own; the parts are better introduced where they do their job, in reel A.

**Decision: two reels.** Each is complete without the other. Reel B opens with a one-sentence recap of the measured
heat, so a viewer who never saw reel A is not lost; nothing in either reel says "see part 1".

## 2. Format and framing (both reels)

- **9:16, 1080 × 1920, 30 fps**, rendered natively with the explainer camera's tall shots (never a crop of 16:9).
- **Safe area:** on-screen text stays inside **x 60–960, y 220–1450** of the 1080 × 1920 frame: clear of the platforms'
  top bar, the right-hand action buttons and the bottom caption/profile area on Reels and Shorts. This is a
  conservative PrayogX rule, to be confirmed against current platform overlays on the first preview.
- **Model first:** the apparatus fills the upper two-thirds; labels sit beside the part they name; equations appear as
  one lower-third card at a time, above y 1450.
- **Captions:** the narration is burned in as short caption lines in the safe area (most viewers watch muted), with an
  `.srt` alongside; added with the narration, not in the silent master.
- **Visual language unchanged** from the approved scenes: calm camera moves, highlight then label, no flashes, no
  question-and-answer beats, no "can you solve" language. The opening states what we will see, as a teacher would.
- **Opening (first 2–3 s):** the title as a clear statement over the apparatus, so the scroll-stopping moment is the
  model itself, not a gimmick. **Ending:** the takeaway card, held for ≈ 2 s, then the PrayogX end card (≈ 1.5 s) with
  the concept page.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| B1 | 6 | wide → system | apparatus at rest | title: **Why a bomb calorimeter measures ΔU** · lower third: **measured: q_V = −C ΔT** | "A bomb calorimeter measures q_V [q V], the heat of a reaction at constant volume, from a temperature rise. | B2 | 7 | wall | still bomb, highlighted | rigid steel walls: they cannot move · lower third: **ΔV = 0** | "The bomb's steel walls are rigid. They cannot move, so the volume stays constant: ΔV [delta V] is zero." | the constant-volume condition |
| B3 | 8 | wall → interior | sample ignites and burns; walls stay put | the gases cannot push the walls outward · **w = 0** | "When the sample burns, the gases cannot push the walls outward, so no expansion work is done: w is zero." | why there is no work |
| B4 | 8 | wall | burnt, settled | lower third: **first law: ΔU = q + w** → **w = 0, so ΔU = q_V** | "The first law says ΔU equals q plus w [delta U equals q plus w]. With w zero, ΔU equals q_V [delta U equals q V]." | first law with w = 0 |
| B5 | 6 + 1.5 | wide → end card | settled | takeaway card: **bomb calorimeter → ΔU = q_V = −C ΔT** · end card | "So a bomb calorimeter measures the change in internal energy: ΔU = q_V = −C ΔT [delta U equals q V equals minus C delta T]." | takeaway |

Note on B1: the opening question ("Why is that ΔU?") states what the reel will explain, as a teacher frames a lesson;
it is not a quiz, and the reel answers it fully.

## 5. Scientific verification per reel

Reel A: V1–V6 and V10 of the explainer spec — rigid sealed bomb, sample + oxygen + electrical ignition, heat through
the steel into water with stirring and insulation, the thermometer measures temperature (ΔT), heat absorbed by the
**whole calorimeter** = C ΔT (NCERT eq. 5.11), q_V = −C ΔT, and ΔT is never called ΔU.
Reel B: V1, V7–V10 — ΔV = 0 because the walls are rigid; w = −p ΔV = 0 (no expansion work; NCERT §5.3(a)); first law
ΔU = q + w; so ΔU = q_V = −C ΔT for the amount burned. The temperatures shown are illustrative; no kJ value appears.
