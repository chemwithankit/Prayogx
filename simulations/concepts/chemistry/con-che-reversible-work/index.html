One Gas, Many Paths: Reversible and Irreversible Work

NCERT Class 11 · Chemistry Part I · Ch 5 Thermodynamics · §5.2.1 Work · an interactive concept experience

01Your experiment

A gas expands from 2 L to 10 L at 25 °C. Does it always do the same work? You will expand it yourself, three different ways, and then push it back.

The gas is NCERT's (Problem 5.2): 2 L at 10 atm, kept at 25 °C by a water bath. Weights on the piston hold it in. The lab guide in the experiment explains what happened, why, and what to do next.

03What the experiment showed

The explanation opens once you have expanded the gas grain by grain and found the reversible work yourself.

  1. Work is an area. When the outside pressure pex stays constant while the volume changes by ΔV, the work done on the gas is w = −pex ΔV (NCERT eq. 5.2). On a p–V plot, the work done by the gas is the area under the outside-pressure line.
  2. All eight weights off at once. The outside pressure falls straight to 2 atm and the gas expands against it from 2 L to 10 L: work by the gas = 2 atm × 8 L = 16.0 L atm
  3. One weight at a time. The gas expands in eight steps, against 9, 8, … 2 atm. Each step is a strip under its own pressure, and the strips add up to more area: w = −Σ p ΔV, work by the gas = 28.6 L atm
  4. Grain by grain. Each grain lowers the outside pressure so little that the gas is always in balance with it. The steps hug the curve pV = 20 L atm and the area approaches its largest possible value, the reversible work: wrev = −2.303 nRT log(Vf/Vi) = −20 L atm × 2.303 × log 5 = −32.2 L atm (NCERT eq. 5.5). With 800 grains the gas did 32.1 L atm.
  5. Back again. Pushing the gas back needs work. All at once, against 10 atm, it costs 80.0 L atm, so the round trip leaves 64.0 L atm in the surroundings. One weight at a time it leaves 8.0 L atm; grain by grain only about 0.1. In general, N equal steps leave (pi − pf)(Vf − Vi)/N = 64/N L atm. Only the reversible path can be undone without leaving anything behind.
  6. Same start, same end, three ways (NCERT Problems 5.2–5.4). The gas starts at 2 L and ends at 10 L each time, at the same temperature, so ΔU = 0 and q = −w:

Reversible process. A process is reversible if it could, at any moment, be reversed by an infinitesimal change. It proceeds infinitely slowly through a series of equilibrium states, with the system and its surroundings always in near equilibrium (NCERT p. 141). All other processes are irreversible.

Key takeaways

  • The work done by a gas is the area under the outside-pressure path on the p–V plot.
  • Same start and end, different path → different work and heat; ΔU is the same.
  • The slower the expansion (the closer pex stays to the gas pressure), the more work the gas does. The reversible path gives the most.
  • On compression the reversible path needs the least work, and a reversible round trip leaves nothing behind.
  • Into a vacuum (pex = 0) the gas does no work.

Notes. NCERT's Problem 5.4 calls this gas "1 mol" and gets 39.4 L atm. But 2 L at 10 atm and 298 K is n = pV/RT = 0.818 mol, so the reversible work for this gas is 32.2 L atm (39.4 L atm is right for 1 mol). The gas is ideal and kept at 25 °C by the water bath. The piston's motion between equilibria is illustrative and not to scale in time; it never changes the work. 1 L atm = 101.325 J. How to use this experience

  1. Read the lab guide in the experiment, then press the big green button. It always names the next thing to do. Twice the experiment asks you to predict first, and the green button waits until you choose with the Predict buttons.
  2. Lift the weights yourself. A weight can only come off when the piston has come to rest, so every step ends in balance.
  3. Pour the sand starts the grain-by-grain run; press it again to stop and look, and once more to go on.
  4. Replay plays your session again from the start, Pause freezes it and Reset starts over. Classroom fills the screen for a projector.
