    Understand the concept

    What are X⁺ and Y⁻?

    PtF6 has one of the highest electron affinities known (≈ 7 eV). It pulls one electron straight off O2.

    O2 + PtF6 → O2+[PtF6]−

    Bond order of X⁺

    O2+ has 15 electrons; the one removed came from an antibonding π* orbital, so the bond gets stronger.

    (10 − 5)/2 = 2.5

    d-electrons of Pt

    In [PtF6]− platinum is +5. Pt is 5d96s1; take away 5 electrons (6s first).

    Pt5+: [Xe]4f14 5d5

    Oxidant or fluorinating agent?

    Only an electron moves: Pt +6 → +5 (reduced), O2 → O2+ (oxidised). All six F stay on Pt — nothing is fluorinated.

    1. Step 1 — Identify X⁺ and Y⁻

      O2(g) + PtF6(g) → O2+[PtF6]−(s)

      The first ionisation energy of O2 (12.07 eV) is low enough for PtF6 to take one electron. X+ = O2+ (dioxygenyl), Y− = [PtF6]− (hexafluoridoplatinate(V)).

      On the bench: the red PtF6 vapour and colourless O2 met and an orange-red crystalline solid appeared as one electron jumped per pair.

    2. Step 2 — Bond order of X⁺ (option A)

      O2+ (15 e⁻): σ1s² σ*1s² σ2s² σ*2s² σ2pz² π2px² π2py² π*2px¹

      Bonding electrons Nb = 10, antibonding Na = 5, bond order = (10 − 5)/2 = 2.5 — not 1.5. (1.5 is the bond order of the superoxide ion O2−, a common trap.) The bond also shortens, 121 → 112 pm. (A) ✗

    3. Step 3 — d-electrons of the metal ion (option B)

      In [PtF6]−: x + 6(−1) = −1, so Pt is +5. Pt (Z = 78) is [Xe]4f145d96s1; removing 6s1 and four 5d electrons leaves 5d5 (octahedral, low spin t2g5). (B) ✓

    4. Step 4 — Oxidant or fluorinating agent? (options C, D)

      Pt goes from +6 in PtF6 to +5 in [PtF6]−: PtF6 gains an electron, it is reduced, so it is the oxidant. (C) ✓

      A fluorinating agent hands F atoms to the other reactant (e.g. U + 3ClF3 → UF6 + 3ClF). Here all six F atoms stay on Pt and no O–F bond forms. (D) ✗

    Independent verification

    • MO counting. The page fills the MO diagram by the aufbau principle and Hund's rule for every species it shows: O2 2.0, O2+ 2.5, O2− 1.5, O22− 1.0, N2 3.0 — the textbook values — and the measured bond lengths fall in the same order.
    • Oxidation states. Charge balance on [PtF6]− and PtF6; one electron gained by Pt equals one electron lost by O2.
    • Electron configuration. Pt [Xe]4f145d96s1 (measured ground state); ions lose 6s before 5d.
    • Solution websites. MathonGo's published solution (their Q39) gives (B), (C) with the same reasoning.
    • This page. Each option is decided from the numbers the lab computes; the answer letters are not written into the page.
    • Answer key. Checked afterwards against the official final key printed with the paper: Q7 → (B), (C).
