    Understand the concept

    How was X identified?

    MnO2 oxidises chloride from concentrated HCl. The only greenish-yellow gas that can form is chlorine.

    Mn: +4 → +2  ·  Cl: −1 → 0  ⇒  X = Cl2

    How was Y identified?

    With chlorine in excess, all three N–H hydrogens of NH3 are replaced by Cl, one after another.

    NH3 → NH2Cl → NHCl2 → NCl3

    How was Z identified?

    Chlorine with excess fluorine at 573 K gives the interhalogen ClF3 (equal volumes at a lower temperature give ClF).

    Cl2 + 3F2 → 2ClF3

    How were the options evaluated?

    (A) Cl2 disinfects water ✓ · (B) NCl3 is pyramidal ✗ · (C) ClF3 makes UF6 for 235U enrichment ✓ · (D) NCl3 is a weaker base than NH3 ✗

    1. Step 1 — Identify X

      MnO2 + 4HCl(conc.) → MnCl2 + Cl2 + 2H2O

      MnO2 (Mn +4) is reduced to Mn2+; chloride (−1) is oxidised to Cl2 (0). Chlorine is the greenish-yellow gas. X = Cl2.

      On the bench: the greenish-yellow gas filled the gas jar and matched only chlorine on the colour chart.

    2. Step 2 — Identify Y

      NH3 + 3Cl2 (excess) → NCl3 + 3HCl

      Y = NCl3. Nitrogen has 3 bond pairs and 1 lone pair: electron geometry tetrahedral, molecular geometry trigonal pyramidal — N sits above the plane of the three Cl atoms. Not planar.

      (With excess NH3 instead, the products are N2 and NH4Cl: 8NH3 + 3Cl2 → N2 + 6NH4Cl — which is why the question says "excess".)

    3. Step 3 — Identify Z

      Cl2 + 3F2 (excess) —573 K→ 2ClF3

      Z = ClF3. Chlorine has 3 bond pairs and 2 lone pairs: trigonal-bipyramidal electron geometry with both lone pairs equatorial, so the molecule is T-shaped.

    4. Step 4 — Evaluate the options

      (A) ✓ Cl2 is widely used as a disinfectant for drinking water.

      (B) ✗ NCl3 is trigonal pyramidal, not planar.

      (C) ✓ ClF3 is a powerful fluorinating agent used to make volatile UF6 — the form in which uranium is handled for 235U enrichment: U + 3ClF3 → UF6 + 3ClF.

      (D) ✗ The electronegative Cl atoms pull electron density away from nitrogen (−I effect), so its lone pair is less available: NCl3 is a weaker Lewis base than NH3.

    Independent verification

    • Balancing. Each equation was balanced by solving its element-conservation equations, and every electron transfer checked by oxidation numbers.
    • VSEPR. Steric numbers from valence electrons: N in NCl3 3 + 1 = 4 (pyramidal, non-planar); Cl in ClF3 3 + 2 = 5 (T-shaped). The page's 3D model puts N about 0.65 Å above the Cl3 plane.
    • Textbook data. NCERT (p-block elements): chlorine is used for sterilising drinking water; ClF3 is used in the production of UF6 for the enrichment of 235U; NCl3 forms from NH3 and excess Cl2.
    • Solution websites. MathonGo's published solution (their Q38) gives (A), (C) with the same identification.
    • This page. Each option is decided from the properties the lab found for the identified compound; the answer letters are not written into the page.
    • Answer key. Checked afterwards against the official final key printed with the paper: Q6 → (A), (C).

    04How to use this simulation

    For a student opening it for the first time. The whole investigation runs by itself after one press.

    STEP 1

    Read the mission brief

    Three reactions hide three compounds X, Y and Z. Which of the four statements about them are correct? More than one can be.

    STEP 2

    Check the conditions

    The question's conditions are loaded — conc. HCl, excess Cl2, excess F2, 573 K. You do not need to change anything.

    STEP 3

    Optional: customize

    Change a condition — dilute HCl, excess NH3, equal volumes of F2. The lab tells you honestly when the question's pathway no longer applies. ↺ RESTORE QUESTION CONDITIONS puts everything back.

    STEP 4

    Start

    Press ▶ START EXPERIMENT. There is nothing else to click (about a minute; 2× makes it faster).

    STEP 5

    Watch

    Gas bubbles off and fills the jar, molecules collide and swap atoms, the temperature climbs to 573 K, each product is built in 3D, and each statement is tested on the analysis board.

    STEP 6

    Answer reveal

    When all four statements are decided the lab says ✓ EXPERIMENT COMPLETE and reveals the answer. Badges unlock on the way.

    STEP 7

    Explore and understand

    Rotate Cl2, NCl3, ClF3 and NH3 in the Molecular explorer, then read the Detailed solution.

    STEP 8

    Experiment again

    Press ▶ REPLAY EXPERIMENT, or change a condition and press ⚙ RUN WITH CUSTOM VALUES. CLASSROOM MODE enlarges everything for a projector.
