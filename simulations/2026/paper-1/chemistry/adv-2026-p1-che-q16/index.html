    Given and asked

    Given

    All four substrates are built on one ring: C1 carries the oxime side chain, C2 (ortho) carries Br, and the NO₂ is on C5 — para to the Br. P and Q are the aldoxime (C–H on C7), R and S the ketoxime (CH₃ on C7). In P, Q and R the oxime O is drawn on the same side as the ring (Z); in S the O-acetyl group points away from the ring (E).

    Asked

    The major product of each reaction — matched to one of five structures, and the one option that gets all four right.

    Understand the concepts

    1 · The geometry of C=N is fixed

    An oxime's C=N does not rotate. Z and E are different compounds. Only the single bond from the ring to C7 turns.

    Z: O syn to the ring → can reach C2 · E: O anti → never

    2 · SNAr needs an activated ring

    A nucleophile adds to the carbon carrying the leaving group; the negative charge must spread onto an electron-withdrawing group ortho or para to it (Meisenheimer complex).

    NO₂ para to Br → activated

    3 · Kemp elimination

    A 1,2-benzisoxazole with an H on C3 is opened by base: C3–H is removed, C≡N forms and the weak N–O bond breaks, giving a 2-cyanophenoxide.

    3-H benzisoxazole + OH⁻ → 2-cyanophenoxide

    4 · O-Acyl oximes

    An O-acetyl aldoxime loses AcOH by anti elimination to a nitrile. An O-acetyl ketoxime has no C–H to lose; aqueous base only removes the acetyl.

    RCH=N–OAc + base → R–C≡N + AcO⁻

      • Solved first. The four products and option (B) were derived before the answer key was opened.
      • Geometry. A separate script builds each oxime with RDKit, relaxes it with the MMFF force field and turns the side chain: the Z oxime O comes to 2.4–2.5 Å of C2, the E oxime O never closer than 4.2 Å (C + O contact 3.22 Å). This page's own model gives 2.1 Å and 4.2 Å.
      • Activation. RDKit's resonance enumeration of the Meisenheimer complex puts the negative charge on the nitro oxygens only when NO₂ is para (or ortho) to the attacked carbon; a meta-nitro control cannot.

      04Concept explanation & key takeaways

      The whole question turns on where the oxime oxygen can reach. Once it is an anion (after base removes its proton), the oxime oxygen is a good nucleophile sitting one bond-turn away from the carbon that carries bromine. If it is on the ring's side of the C=N bond (Z) it can swing into contact and, because the nitro group para to bromine can take the negative charge, it displaces bromide and closes a five-membered ring — a 1,2-benzisoxazole. What happens next depends on C3: with a hydrogen there (the aldoxime, P) base tears the ring open again to a nitrile and a phenoxide (the Kemp elimination); with a methyl there (R) the ring stays. Acetylating the oxygen first (Q) takes the nucleophile away and turns the C–H into the thing base removes — a nitrile, with bromine untouched. And an oxygen that points the wrong way (S) can never reach, so mild base only cleaves the ester.

      C=N geometry decides reach. Z oximes of o-halo aryl carbonyls can cyclise to 1,2-benzisoxazoles; E oximes cannot.
      SNAr needs ortho/para activation. The NO₂ para to Br carries the Meisenheimer charge; move it meta and nothing happens.
      3-H benzisoxazoles do not survive base. The Kemp elimination gives 2-cyanophenoxides (yellow with a para-NO₂); 3-substituted ones survive.
      O-Acyl aldoximes → nitriles. Anti elimination of AcOH; the same group on a ketoxime can only be hydrolysed off.
      (3) is a Beckmann product. It needs acid and the aryl anti to the leaving O — not the basic conditions in this question.
      Match structures, not names. (5) and R's substrate differ only in C=N geometry; that difference is the whole of S.
