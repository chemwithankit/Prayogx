QThe question — verbatim

JEE (Advanced) 2026, Paper 1, Chemistry, Section 1 — single correct option, +3 for the correct option, 0 if unanswered, −1 otherwise.

Q.1  An ideal gas (0.5 mol), initially at 2 bar pressure, is compressed at a constant temperature of 600 K in two steps: first, against a constant external pressure of P bar (2 < P < 8), and then against constant external pressure of 8 bar. At each step, the compression is stopped only when the pressure of the gas becomes equal to the external pressure. The total work done on the gas in these steps is W. Considering all possible values of P (2 < P < 8) and taking the gas constant as R (in J K−1 mol−1), the minimum value of |W| (in J) is

  • (A)207R
  • (B)600R
  • (C)630R
  • (D)900R
What is being asked. P is not given — it is free. Every choice of P between 2 and 8 bar gives a different total work, and the question wants the smallest. So the experiment below does what a chemist would: it tries the compression at many values of P, measures the work each time, and finds the cheapest.

    02Detailed solution

    Run the experiment — the solution opens with the answer.

    The solution is still locked

    The experiment has not finished yet. Press START and let it run to the end — or use Reveal anyway in the header if you are revising.

    1. Given

      n = 0.5 mol,   T = 600 K (constant),   P1 = 2 bar → P2 = 8 bar,   first step against P (2 < P < 8), second against 8 bar.

      Find the smallest |W| over all P, as a multiple of R.

    2. Concept — work against a constant external pressure

      When a piston is pushed by a constant external pressure, the work done on the gas is that pressure times the volume swept, whatever the gas does inside:

      won gas = −Pext ΔV = Pext(Vinitial − Vfinal)

      Each step stops when the gas pressure equals Pext; the bath returns the gas to 600 K, so the end volume of each step is on the isotherm, V = nRT/P.

      In the experiment this was the shaded rectangle on the indicator diagram: the height is the load on the piston, the width is how far it went down.

    3. Step 1 and step 2

      w1 = P(nRT/P1 − nRT/P) = nRT(P/P1 − 1)
      w2 = P2(nRT/P − nRT/P2) = nRT(P2/P − 1)
      W(P) = w1 + w2 = nRT(P/P1 + P2/P − 2) = 300R(P/2 + 8/P − 2)
      A small P makes step 1 cheap but leaves a long, heavy step 2; a large P does the opposite. That is why the W–P graph in the experiment came out U-shaped.

    4. Find the minimum

      dW/dP = nRT(1/P1 − P2/P²) = 0  ⇒  P = √(P1P2) = √(2 × 8) = 4 bar

      The second derivative 2nRT P2/P³ is positive, so it is a minimum. At P = 4 bar both steps cost the same, nRT(2 − 1) = 300R each — the experiment's best run showed two equal rectangles.

    5. Minimum work

      Wmin = 2nRT(√(P2/P1) − 1) = 2 × 0.5 × 600 × (√4 − 1) R = 600R J  (≈ 4988.7 J with R = 8.314)

      The work is done on the gas, so it is positive; |W| = 600R. Because the gas is ideal and isothermal, ΔU = 0 and the same 600R J leaves as heat to the bath.

      Answer: —

    6. Why the other options are wrong

      (A) 207R is nRT ln 2 — only half the reversible work nRT ln 4 = 415.9R. No process at all, with any number of steps, can compress the gas with less than the reversible work, so 207R is impossible. (C) 630R is a real two-step run, but at P = 3.2 or 5 bar — not the minimum. (D) 900R is the limit P → 2 or P → 8, i.e. a single step straight against 8 bar.

      415.9R (reversible)  <  600R (best two-step)  <  900R (one step)

    Independent verification

    • Symbolic. A computer-algebra system built W(P) from w = −PextΔV, found the single stationary point P = √(P1P2), confirmed d²W/dP² > 0 there, and gave Wmin = 600R.
    • Brute force. A 60 000-point scan of P, using volumes in litres and bar (1 bar L = 100 J) and no formula for W, found the minimum at 4.000 bar and 600.000R = 4988.7 J.
    • Exact. In fractions, step 1 = 300R and step 2 = 300R at P = 4 bar — equal, as the symmetry of W in ln P requires.
    • Bounds. The reversible work nRT ln 4 = 415.9R lies below 600R and the one-step work 900R above it; more equal-ratio steps (1, 2, 4, 100) give 900, 600, 497, 419R, closing in on 415.9R.
    • Distractor audit. 207R is below the reversible limit; 630R solves W(P) at P = 3.2 or 5 bar; 900R is the one-step limit. Only 600R is the minimum.
    • This page. The experiment measures each run as Pext × volume swept, finds the lowest run and refines around it; the option letter is chosen at run time by matching the measured minimum against the printed options — it is not written into the page.
    • Answer key. Checked afterwards against the official final key printed with the paper: Q1 → (B).

    03How to use this simulation

    For a student opening it for the first time. The whole experiment runs by itself after one press.

    STEP 1

    Read the question

    A gas is squeezed from 2 bar to 8 bar in two pushes. The first push can be any pressure P between them. Which P needs the least work, and how much work is that?

    STEP 2

    Check the values

    The question's values — 0.5 mol, 600 K, 2 bar, 8 bar — are already filled in. You do not need to enter anything.

    STEP 3

    Optional: change a value

    Change any one value, or several. The rest stay at the question's values. An empty box means "use the question value". The badge switches to ⚙ USING CUSTOM VALUES; ↺ RESET TO QUESTION VALUES puts everything back.

    STEP 4

    Start

    Press ▶ START EXPERIMENT. There is nothing else to click — the experiment runs from beginning to end on its own (about 45 seconds; 2× makes it faster).

    STEP 5

    Watch

    A weight lands on the piston, the gas is squeezed, molecules crowd together and hit the piston more often, heat flows out into the bath, and the work shows up as a shaded area on the indicator diagram. Then the experiment repeats this for many values of P and plots the total work for each — watch the lowest point appear.

    STEP 6

    Answer reveal

    When the experiment finishes it says ✓ EXPERIMENT COMPLETE and reveals the answer. The header answer box unlocks at the same time.

    STEP 7

    Understand

    Scroll to Detailed solution. Each step points back to what you saw in the experiment.

    STEP 8

    Experiment again

    Press ▶ REPLAY EXPERIMENT, or change a value — try 0.25 bar more at the start, or 16 bar at the end — and press ⚙ RUN WITH CUSTOM VALUES. Watch where the best P lands each time — there is a pattern. CLASSROOM MODE enlarges everything for a projector.
