QThe question — verbatim

JEE (Advanced) 2026, Paper 1, Chemistry, Section 1 — single correct option, +3 for the correct option, 0 if unanswered, −1 otherwise.

Q.4  Considering LiBH4 reduces an ester group to the corresponding alcohol and does not reduce a carboxylic acid group, the correct statement about the major products P, Q, R and S is

  • (A)P & Q are identical, and R & S are diastereomers.
  • (B)P & Q are diastereomers, and R & S are identical.
  • (C)P & Q are diastereomers, and R & S are diastereomers.
  • (D)P & Q are identical, and R & S are identical.
What is being asked. Each substrate carries two different arms on the same ring carbon — an ester arm (wedge, toward you) and an acid arm (hash, away from you). LiBH4 reduces only the ester; BH3 reduces only the acid. Either way the new CH2OH finds the other arm's C=O, and the acid workup (H+) closes a six-membered lactone (a cyclic ester). The two reagents close that ring in opposite directions. Identical means the two products can be turned to lie exactly on top of each other; enantiomers are non-superimposable mirror images; diastereomers are stereoisomers that are neither. The experiment makes all four lactones and tests exactly that.

    02Detailed solution

    Run the experiment — the solution opens with the answer.

    The solution is still locked

    The experiment has not finished yet. Press START and let it run to the end — or use Reveal anyway in the header if you are revising.

    1. What each reagent touches

      LiBH4 reduces the ester (given in the question) and leaves the acid alone. BH3 is the classic reagent for the opposite job: it reduces a carboxylic acid quickly and an ester only slowly. So the two routes reduce different arms of the same molecule.

      LiBH4:  C1–CH2CO2Et (wedge) → C1–CH2CH2OH,   C1–CH2CO2H (hash) unchanged
      BH3:  C1–CH2CO2H (hash) → C1–CH2CH2OH,   C1–CH2CO2Et (wedge) unchanged
      In the experiment the hydride went to the ester carbon in the LiBH4 flask and to the acid carbon in the BH3 flask.

    2. H+ closes a δ-lactone

      In each product a CH2CH2OH and a CH2C(=O)X sit on the same carbon. Counting O–CH2–CH2–C1–CH2–C(=O) gives a six-membered ring, so acid catalyses ring closure (loss of H2O from the hydroxy acid, or of EtOH from the hydroxy ester) to a spiro δ-lactone. All four products have the same formula, C9H14O2, and the same connectivity in each pair.

      Without the ring closure P and Q would not even have the same formula (an acid versus an ethyl ester) — the lactone is what makes the comparison in the options possible.

    3. The ring closes in opposite directions

      P and R (LiBH4): the ring O comes from the wedge arm, the C=O from the hash arm.
      Q and S (BH3): the ring O comes from the hash arm, the C=O from the wedge arm.

      In the lactone the spiro carbon C1 carries two different ring branches (–CH2–C(=O)– and –CH2–CH2–O–), so swapping which face each branch is on inverts C1. The CH3 carbon is untouched by either reagent.

    4. Top pair: P and Q

      C1 and the CH3 carbon next to it are both stereocentres. One is inverted, the other is not: P and Q are diastereomers — both chiral, not mirror images. (CIP: the CH3 carbon is R in both; the spiro carbon is R in P and S in Q — one descriptor changes, not both.)

      On the bench: P on Q left mismatched atoms, and the mirror image of Q did not fit either.

    5. Bottom pair: R and S

      With the CH3 directly across the ring, a mirror plane runs through C1, the CH3 carbon and the lactone ring, so R and S are each achiral. They differ in whether the C=O branch sits on the same face as the CH3 (cis) or the opposite face (trans): R and S are cis/trans diastereomers.

      On the bench: R on S did not fit; the mirror image of S is S itself, so the mirror test gave the same mismatch — and the control showed R fits its own mirror image exactly.
      Answer: —

    6. Why the other options are wrong

      (A), (B) and (D) each call a pair "identical". That would need the two routes to give the same lactone, which happens only if C1 is not a stereocentre in the product — for example with no CH3 on the ring at all. Remove the CH3 from a substrate in the experiment and that pair does come out identical. Neither pair can be enantiomers: only C1 is inverted, never the CH3 carbon.

    Independent verification

    • From the drawing. The two substrates were entered into RDKit with 2D coordinates copied from the printed structures and their wedge and hash bonds; RDKit assigned the stereocentres itself.
    • Reactions. Ester-only and acid-only reductions and the lactonisation were run as reaction templates that never touch a bond to a stereocentre. P ≠ Q and neither is the mirror image of the other; R ≠ S and each is its own mirror image — two diastereomer pairs.
    • CIP. CH3 carbon R in both P and Q; spiro carbon R in P, S in Q — one centre inverted. R (r, r) and S (s, s): pseudo-asymmetric centres, cis/trans.
    • Enumeration. The top lactone has four stereoisomers; P and Q come from different enantiomer pairs. The bottom one has two, cis and trans — exactly R and S.
    • 3D. Twenty force-field conformers of each, aligned over every symmetry-equivalent atom mapping: P on a fresh copy of P 0.000 Å, P on Q 1.27 Å, P on the mirror image of Q 0.41 Å, R on S 0.30 Å.
    • Control. With no CH3, both routes give the same lactone.
    • This page. The overlay bench finds the best rotation by least squares (Horn's quaternion method) and names each pair from the RMSDs; the option letter is matched at run time and is not written into the page.
    • Answer key. Checked afterwards against the official final key printed with the paper: Q4 → (C).

    03How to use this simulation

    For a student opening it for the first time. The whole experiment runs by itself after one press.

    STEP 1

    Read the question

    One substrate, two reagents. LiBH4 gives P (or R); BH3 gives Q (or S). Are the two products the same molecule, mirror images, or something else?

    STEP 2

    Check the values

    The printed structures — CH3 next to the quaternary carbon on top, across the ring below, both on a wedge — are already set. You do not need to change anything.

    STEP 3

    Optional: change a value

    Move either CH3, flip it to a hash, or remove it. The badge switches to ⚙ USING CUSTOM VALUES; ↺ RESET TO QUESTION VALUES puts everything back.

    STEP 4

    Start

    Press ▶ START EXPERIMENT. There is nothing else to click — the experiment runs from beginning to end on its own (under a minute; 2× makes it faster).

    STEP 5

    Watch

    Hydride hits one arm, the new OH swings round and closes the lactone, and the leaving group drifts away. Then the two products are laid on top of each other: green rings mark atoms that fit, red rings atoms that do not. Then the second one is reflected and tried again.

    STEP 6

    Answer reveal

    After both pairs are classified the experiment says ✓ EXPERIMENT COMPLETE and reveals the answer. The header answer box unlocks at the same time.

    STEP 7

    Understand

    Scroll to Detailed solution. Each step points back to what you saw in the experiment.

    STEP 8

    Experiment again

    Press ▶ REPLAY EXPERIMENT, or set the top CH3 to none and press ⚙ RUN WITH CUSTOM VALUES — which option would be right then? CLASSROOM MODE enlarges everything for a projector.
