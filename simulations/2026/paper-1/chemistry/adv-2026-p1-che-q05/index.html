QThe question — verbatim

JEE (Advanced) 2026, Paper 1, Chemistry, Section 2 — one or more options correct: +4 only if all the correct options are chosen, partial marks +3 / +2 / +1 for a correct subset, 0 if unanswered, −1 otherwise.

Q.5  The 2s and the 2p orbital energies of hydrogen atom are E2s(H) and E2p(H), respectively. The 2s and the 2p orbital energies of lithium atom are E2s(Li) and E2p(Li), respectively. The correct option(s) about the orbital energies is(are)

  • (A)E2s(Li) < E2p(Li)
  • (B)E2s(H) = E2p(H)
  • (C)E2p(H) < E2s(Li)
  • (D)E2s(H) > E2s(Li)
What is being asked. An orbital energy is the energy of an electron in that orbital — negative, because the electron is bound; more negative means more tightly held. In hydrogen the electron is alone with a +1 nucleus. In lithium the 2s or 2p electron shares the atom with two 1s core electrons that partly shield the +3 nucleus. How much shielding an electron feels depends on how often it gets inside the core — its penetration. The experiment solves for all four energies, measures the penetration of each orbital and then tests every option.

    02Detailed solution

    Run the experiment — the solution opens with the answer.

    The solution is still locked

    The experiment has not finished yet. Press START and let it run to the end — or use Reveal anyway in the header if you are revising.

    1. Hydrogen — energy depends only on n

      Hydrogen has one electron and nothing to shield it, so its energy is fixed by the principal quantum number alone:

      En = −13.6 / n² eV  ⇒  E2s(H) = E2p(H) = −13.6/4 = −3.40 eV

      The 2s and 2p orbitals of hydrogen are degenerate. (B) is correct.

      In the experiment the solver landed on exactly the same energy for H 2s and H 2p, and both had Zeff = 1.00.

    2. Lithium — energy depends on n and l (penetration)

      In lithium the outer electron moves in the field of the +3 nucleus and the two 1s electrons. Outside the core it feels about +1; inside the core it feels up to +3. The 2s orbital has a small inner lobe that penetrates the core; the 2p orbital, with its angular node at the nucleus, stays mostly outside.

      2s spends more time inside the core  ⇒  higher Zeff  ⇒  lower energy:  E2s(Li) < E2p(Li)

      (A) is correct.

      On the bench: the orange dots (electron found inside the core) were far more frequent for Li 2s than for Li 2p, and the P(r) graph showed the 2s inner bump sitting in the shaded core.

    3. Hydrogen against lithium

      The orbital energy follows E ≈ −13.6 Zeff²/n² eV. For H, Zeff = 1. For the 2s electron of Li, the nuclear charge is 3 and the 1s² core does not shield it completely, so Zeff > 1 (about 1.26 from the measured energy; about 1.3 by Slater-type estimates).

      Zeff(Li, 2s) > Zeff(H, 2s)  ⇒  E2s(Li) < E2s(H)  ⇒  E2s(H) > E2s(Li)

      (D) is correct. And since E2p(H) = E2s(H), also E2p(H) > E2s(Li) — the reverse of what (C) says. (C) is wrong.

    4. Numbers

      —

      Measured (spectroscopy): H 2s = 2p = −3.40 eV; Li 2s = −5.39 eV (minus the ionisation energy); Li 2p = −3.54 eV (the 2s → 2p red line of lithium, 671 nm, is 1.85 eV). The simple core model misses exchange with the core, so it under-binds Li 2s a little — but every inequality in the options comes out the same.

      E2s(Li) < E2p(Li) < E2s(H) = E2p(H)
      Answer: —

    Independent verification

    • Numerical Schrödinger equation. The radial equation was solved by Numerov shooting on a logarithmic grid, separately from the page: H 2s = H 2p = −3.401 eV (the exact −13.6/4); Li 2s = −4.76 eV, Li 2p = −3.43 eV in the field of the nucleus plus a 1s² core (Slater exponent Z − 5/16).
    • Spectroscopic data. H n = 2 levels −3.40 eV; Li ionisation energy 5.39 eV; Li resonance line 670.8 nm (1.85 eV) → Li 2p −3.54 eV. Same order; (A), (B), (D) true, (C) false.
    • Slater's rules. Li 2s: σ = 2 × 0.85 = 1.70, Zeff = 1.30 → E ≈ −13.6 × 1.30²/4 = −5.75 eV < −3.40 eV.
    • Robustness. With the core removed (Z = 3, no 1s electrons) the 2s and 2p become degenerate again and (A) turns false; for Be⁺, B²⁺ … the same three options stay true.
    • Solution websites. MathonGo's published solution (their Q37) gives (A), (B), (D) with the same reasoning — degeneracy in H, penetration in Li, Zeff(Li, 2s) ≈ 1.3 > 1.
    • This page. The energies come from the page's own solver and the true options are found by comparing them at run time; the answer is not written into the page.
    • Answer key. Checked afterwards against the official final key printed with the paper: Q5 → (A), (B), (D).

    03How to use this simulation

    For a student opening it for the first time. The whole experiment runs by itself after one press.

    STEP 1

    Read the question

    Four energies — 2s and 2p of hydrogen, 2s and 2p of lithium. Which of the four statements about them are true? More than one can be.

    STEP 2

    Check the values

    The question's atoms are loaded: hydrogen (Z = 1) and lithium (Z = 3, two 1s core electrons). You do not need to enter anything.

    STEP 3

    Optional: change a value

    Change the second atom's nuclear charge or the number of core electrons. An empty box means "use the question value". The badge switches to ⚙ USING CUSTOM VALUES; ↺ RESET TO QUESTION VALUES puts everything back.

    STEP 4

    Start

    Press ▶ START EXPERIMENT. There is nothing else to click — the experiment runs from beginning to end on its own (about 40 seconds; 2× makes it faster).

    STEP 5

    Watch

    For each orbital the solver homes in on the energy, the electron cloud appears — orange dots are the moments the electron is inside the 1s core — and the energy drops onto the ladder. Then each option is tested against the ladder: ✓ or ✗.

    STEP 6

    Answer reveal

    When the experiment finishes it says ✓ EXPERIMENT COMPLETE and reveals the correct options. The header answer box unlocks at the same time.

    STEP 7

    Understand

    Scroll to Detailed solution. Each step points back to what you saw in the experiment.

    STEP 8

    Experiment again

    Press ▶ REPLAY EXPERIMENT, or set the core electrons to 0 and press ⚙ RUN WITH CUSTOM VALUES — what happens to the gap between 2s and 2p? CLASSROOM MODE enlarges everything for a projector.
