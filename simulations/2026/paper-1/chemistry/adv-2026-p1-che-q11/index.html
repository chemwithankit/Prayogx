    Understand the concept

    Count every C=O

    A ketone, an aldehyde, an acid, an ester and an anhydride all contain >C=O. Each reactant starts with 3.

    3 and 3

    β-Keto acids lose CO₂

    A CO₂H on the carbon next to a C=O leaves as CO₂ on heating, through a six-membered cyclic transition state, giving an enol.

    RCOCH(R')CO₂H → RCOCH₂R' + CO₂

    cis-1,2-Diacids lose water

    Two CO₂H on neighbouring carbons, on the same face, close a five-membered cyclic anhydride on heating.

    diacid → anhydride + H₂O

    Add them up

    X keeps only its ketone; Y keeps its ketone and gains two anhydride C=O from the two acids.

    1 + 3

    1. Step 1 — Reaction 1 is a double β-keto acid

      HO₂C–CH(CH₃)–CO–CH(CH₃)–CO₂H: each CO₂H is on a carbon α to the ketone
      On the bench: the flask fizzed and the limewater turned milky — CO₂, twice.

    2. Step 2 — The mechanism, twice

      Three curved arrows around a six-membered ring: the O–H electrons form the second C=O of CO₂, the C–CO₂H bond becomes the C=C of an enol, the ketone π pair picks up the H. The enol then tautomerises (H⁺ on carbon, H⁺ off oxygen) back to a ketone — which is again next to a CO₂H, so it happens a second time.

      X = CH₃CH₂COCH₂CH₃ (pentan-3-one), C₅H₁₀O: 1 C=O

    3. Step 3 — Reaction 2 is a cis-1,2-diacid, not a β-keto acid

      Its CO₂H groups hang on C3 and C4, two bonds away from the ketone carbon — no cyclic transition state for CO₂ loss. But the two acids sit side by side on the same face (the wedged ring bonds), so one OH oxygen attacks the other C=O carbon, a proton moves, and water leaves: a five-membered cyclic anhydride.

      Y = cis-fused keto anhydride, C₇H₆O₄: 1 ketone + 2 anhydride = 3 C=O

    4. Step 4 — Add

      1 + 3 = —

      The water that leaves is made from an OH oxygen and two H — no C=O is lost. CO₂ is a gas that leaves the flask: it is not part of X.

    5. Step 5 — The traps

      Stopping X after one decarboxylation gives 2 (sum 5); counting the two CO₂ as part of X gives 8; letting Y lose CO₂ too, or letting the water "take a C=O", both undercount. A trans diacid could not close the ring — though, still holding three C=O, the sum would be 4 anyway.

    Independent verification

    • The drawing. The wedged ring bonds were rebuilt as a mol block and read by RDKit: the two CO₂H are cis (meso diacid).
    • Reaction templates. RDKit templates for β-keto acid decarboxylation (repeated until none is left) and 1,2-diacid dehydration generate X = pentan-3-one and Y = the cis-fused anhydride — and do not fire where they should not.
    • Counting. SMARTS counts 1 C=O in X and 3 in Y; atoms balance for both reactions.
    • Strain. MMFF puts the trans-fused anhydride about 17 kcal/mol above the cis-fused one — why only the cis acid closes.
    • Solution websites. MathonGo's published solution (their Q43) gives 4.
    • This page. Products come from pushing the arrows on the molecule graphs; every step is checked for valence and charge; the count is made at run time.
    • Answer key. Checked afterwards against the official final key printed with the paper: Q11 → 4.

    STEP 6

    When the audit is complete the lab says ✓ EXPERIMENT COMPLETE and reveals the sum. Badges unlock on the way.

    STEP 7

    Explore and understand

    In the Mechanism explorer step through either reaction and drag through each step. Then read the Detailed solution.

    STEP 8

    Experiment again
