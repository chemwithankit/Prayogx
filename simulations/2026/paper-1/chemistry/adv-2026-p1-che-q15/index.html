    Understand the concept

    Ozonolysis opens both rings

    The only C=C is the ring-fusion bond. Cutting it turns a bicyclic alkene into one ten-membered ring with two C=O groups.

    6-6 → cyclodecane-1,6-dione · 7-5 → cyclodecane-1,5-dione

    Zn / H₂O keeps ketones

    Reductive work-up cuts the ozonide's O–O bond without oxidising further: two ketones, and ZnO.

    ozonide + Zn → 2 C=O + ZnO

    More stable enolate

    The more substituted enolate (from the CH that carries CH₃) is the more stable one — but it must be able to close a ring of five or more atoms.

    C=C with more alkyl groups = more stable

    Intramolecular aldol

    The enolate carbon attacks the other C=O across the ring: one new C–C bond makes two rings, a β-hydroxy ketone with OH on a ring-fusion carbon.

    6-6 → 5-7  ·  5-7 → 6-6

    3. P — enolate, aldol, protonation

      width='360px' height='250px' viewBox='0 0 360 250'>
      Step 1 — 1,3-dipolar cycloaddition: ozone's O⁻ lone pair and the C=C π pair form two C–O bonds while the O=O⁺ π pair falls back onto the central oxygen — the molozonide (1,2,3-trioxolane).
    Step 2 — retro-cycloaddition: a ring-O lone pair makes C=O⁺, the ring-fusion C–C bond breaks into C=O and the O–O bond breaks: a ketone and a carbonyl oxide on one ten-membered ring.
    Step 3 — recombination: the carbonyl oxide O⁻ adds to the ketone carbon and the ketone O bonds to the other carbon — the secondary ozonide (1,2,4-trioxolane) bridging the ring.
    Step 4 — zinc gives an electron pair to a peroxide oxygen; the weak O–O bond breaks.
    Step 5 — the O⁻ lone pair forms C=O, the bridging O takes the C–O pair and forms the second C=O, releasing ZnO: cyclodecane-1,6-dione (from a 6-6 alkene).
    Step 6 — OH⁻ removes the α-H on the more substituted carbon (the CH bearing CH₃): the more stable, more substituted enolate.
    Step 7 — intramolecular aldol: the enolate carbon attacks the other C=O; the new C–C bond closes a five-membered and a seven-membered ring.
    Step 8 — the alkoxide takes a proton from water and OH⁻ is regenerated: the β-hydroxy ketone.
    P → the 5-7 fused β-hydroxy ketone (new C–C bond highlighted): CH₃ and C=O at one ring-fusion carbon side, OH at the other with the second CH₃ beside it in the seven-membered ring — List-II (2).
    P: dione ring a–C1(Me)–C2–C3–C4–b–C5(Me)–C6–C7–C8 · enolate at C1 (CH–CH₃, more substituted) attacks C=O at b
    new rings: C1–C2–C3–C4–b (5) and C1–a–C8–C7–C6–C5–b (7)  ⇒  5-7 product, CH₃ + C=O beside one fusion carbon, OH + CH₃ at the other  ⇒  (2)

  1. Q, R and S — the same eight steps; only the ring closure differs

    Q: ozonolysis gives cyclodecane-1,5-dione; the more stable enolate closes rings of 6 and 6 atoms → List-II (1). The other CH(CH₃) enolate would have to close a 4-membered ring, so it cannot react.
    R: ozonolysis gives cyclodecane-1,5-dione; the more stable enolate closes rings of 6 and 6 atoms → List-II (5).
    S: ozonolysis gives cyclodecane-1,6-dione; the more stable enolate closes rings of 5 and 7 atoms → List-II (3).

    3. Independent verification

    • Arrow-pushing, twice. The page and a separate Python script push the same eight steps; every intermediate has valid valences and the total charge is kept.
    • RDKit. Reaction SMARTS for ozonolysis and aldol, applied independently, give the same four products; canonical SMILES match List-II (2), (1), (5), (3).
    • Enolate audit. For each dione every α-C–H enolate is listed with the ring it would close; only the chosen ones are both most substituted and able to close rings of 5–7.
    • Solution websites. MathonGo's published solution (their Q47) gives (C).
    • This page. Products are made by pushing the arrows; the match is found by comparing graphs with the printed structures; nothing is written in.
