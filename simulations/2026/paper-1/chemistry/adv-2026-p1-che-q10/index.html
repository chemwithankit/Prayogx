The mystery. One metal, four corners, three ligands. Thiocyanate can grab the metal with its N or its S; nitrite with its N or an O; glycinate grips with an N and an O at once. Some ways of building the complex are truly different molecules, others are the same molecule turned round. The lab builds every one, turns each one every way a square allows, holds each up to a mirror — and counts what is left.

    Understand the concept

    Who is inside the brackets

    K⁺ is a counter-ion. The complex anion [M(NCS)(NO₂)(gly)]⁻ has M²⁺ and four donor atoms: one each from NCS⁻ and NO₂⁻, two from glycinate.

    x − 1 − 1 − 1 = −1 ⇒ M(II), CN 4

    Linkage isomerism

    NCS⁻ binds through N or S; NO₂⁻ through N (nitro) or O (nitrito). The choices are independent.

    2 × 2 = 4 sets

    Geometric isomerism

    Glycinate spans two cis corners (its N and O are different). The two other ligands fill the remaining corners: one of them trans to N, the other trans to O — or the other way round.

    [M(AB)cd]: 2 isomers

    Optical isomerism

    A square-planar complex lies in its own mirror plane, so its mirror image is itself.

    0 optical isomers

    1. Step 1 — Donor atoms

      NCS⁻: M–NCS or M–SCN ;  NO₂⁻: M–NO₂ or M–ONO ;  gly⁻: N and O (unsymmetrical bidentate, AB)
      On the bench: each ambidentate ligand was shown bound through each of its two ends.

    2. Step 2 — Linkage sets

      2 (NCS/SCN) × 2 (NO₂/ONO) = 4

    3. Step 3 — Geometric isomers for each set

      [M(AB)cd], square planar: c trans to A (d trans to B) or c trans to B (d trans to A) ⇒ 2

      The lab built all 16 seatings of each set (4 cis edges × 2 ways round × 2 ways to fill the other corners) and found that the 8 rotations of a square fold them into exactly 2.

    4. Step 4 — Mirror images

      Every isomer is flat, so it is superimposable on its mirror image: no optical isomers.

      Total = 4 × 2 = —

    5. Step 5 — The traps

      Adding instead of multiplying gives 6; doubling for mirror images gives 16; forgetting linkage isomerism gives 2; treating glycinate as symmetrical gives 4. On a tetrahedron the same ligands would also give 8 — but as 4 pairs of enantiomers, a different reason entirely.

    Independent verification

    • Brute force. A separate Python script builds all 64 seatings and folds them with the 8 symmetry operations of a square: 8 isomers.
    • Burnside's lemma. Counting only fixed seatings: (1/8) × 64 = 8 — every isomer has exactly 8 equivalent seatings.
    • 3-D geometry. The rotations were found numerically from coordinates, and each isomer's reflected coordinates were superimposed on the original: none is chiral.
    • Solution websites. MathonGo's published solution (their Q42) gives 8.
    • This page. The count is made at run time by the page's own enumeration; it is not written into the page.
    • Answer key. Checked afterwards against the official final key printed with the paper: Q10 → 8.

    04How to use this simulation

    For a student opening it for the first time. The whole investigation runs by itself after one press.

    STEP 1

    Read the mission brief

    A square-planar complex with glycinate, thiocyanate and nitrite. How many different isomers can it have?

    STEP 2

    Check the values

    The question's complex is loaded — square planar, NCS⁻, NO₂⁻, glycinate. You do not need to change anything.

    STEP 3

    Optional: customize

    Try a tetrahedral metal, a symmetrical chelate or single-donor halides. The lab tells you honestly when a change takes you off the question. ↺ RESTORE QUESTION CONDITIONS puts everything back.

    STEP 4

    Start

    Press ▶ START EXPERIMENT. There is nothing else to click (about a minute; 2× makes it faster).

    STEP 5

    Watch

    The ligands show their donor ends, the lab lists the linkage sets, seats the chelate, builds all 64 seatings one by one, folds them by symmetry and holds each isomer to a mirror.

    STEP 6

    Answer reveal

    When the audit is complete the lab says ✓ EXPERIMENT COMPLETE and reveals the count. Badges unlock on the way.

    STEP 7

    Explore and understand

    In the Isomer explorer turn, flip and swap until you have made every isomer yourself, then press SHOW ALL ISOMERS. Then read the Detailed solution.

    STEP 8

    Experiment again

    Press ▶ REPLAY EXPERIMENT, or change a ligand and press ⚙ RUN WITH CUSTOM VALUES. CLASSROOM MODE enlarges everything for a projector.
