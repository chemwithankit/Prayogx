Advanced kinetics — how fast it settles

The plateau is set by the ratio of the rate constants; how quickly the reaction gets there is set by their sum. Take d[P]/dt = kf[R] − kb[P] with [R] = [R]0 − [P]:

d[P]/dt = kf[R]0 − (kf + kb)[P]
kobserved = kf + kb = 5 s⁻¹  (= 5kf)
τ = 1/(kf + kb) = 0.200 s  ·  half-way in 0.139 s
K = kf/kb = [P]eq/[R]eq = 0.25

So the distance still to go, [P]eq − [P], shrinks as e−(kf+kb)t: a straight line on a log scale, and its slope measures kf + kb — which is how you would get both rate constants from one experiment.

The solution is still locked

  1. At equilibrium the two rates are equal — not zero

    Both directions are elementary first-order steps, so each rate is its rate constant times the concentration it uses up. Equilibrium is the moment the two become equal.

    rf = kf[R],   rb = kb[P]   ⇒   kf[R]eq = kb[P]eq

  2. Put in kb = 4kf

    kf[R]eq = 4kf[P]eq   ⇒   [R]eq = 4[P]eq

    The backward step is four times faster per molecule, so at balance there must be four times as much R as P for the two rates to match.

  3. Nothing is created or destroyed

    Every R lost becomes a P, so the total is always the starting amount.

    [R]eq + [P]eq = [R]0   ⇒   4[P]eq + [P]eq = [R]0   ⇒   5[P]eq = [R]0
    [P]eq/[R]0 = 0.2   and   [R]eq/[R]0 = 0.8

  4. The shape of the curves

    Substituting [R] = [R]0 − [P] into the rate law and integrating from [P] = 0:

    d[P]/dt = kf[R]0 − (kf + kb)[P]
    [P]/[R]0 = kf/(kf + kb) (1 − e−(kf+kb)t) = 0.2(1 − e−5kft)
    [R]/[R]0 = 0.8 + 0.2 e−5kft

    So [R]/[R]0 starts at 1 and [P]/[R]0 at 0, both change fastest at the start, both bend over smoothly, their sum is always 1, and they level off at 0.8 and 0.2 after a few multiples of 1/(5kf).

  5. Hold each graph against that

    (A) levels off at 0.5 and 0.5 — that needs kb = kf. (B) levels off with P at 0.8 — that needs kf = 4kb, the rate constants the wrong way round. (D) never levels off, its [P] curve bends upward, and where its curves cross [R] + [P] is about 0.6 of [R]0, not 1. (C) starts at 1 and 0, bends over, and settles at 0.8 and 0.2.

Independent verification

  • Symbolic. The rate law d[P]/dt = kf([R]0 − [P]) − kb[P] was solved from scratch by a computer-algebra system with [P](0) = 0; its solution is exactly the expression above, and its limit as t → ∞ is 1/5 of [R]0.
  • Numerical. A hand-written Runge–Kutta integrator, using no closed form at all, agrees with that expression to 4 × 10⁻¹⁵ at seven different times, and keeps [R] + [P] = [R]0 to 2 × 10⁻¹⁵.
  • Exact. In rational arithmetic, kf/(kf + kb) = 1/5 and kf·(4/5) = kb·(1/5) with kb = 4kf — detailed balance holds exactly, and K = [P]eq/[R]eq = 1/4 = kf/kb.
  • Molecules. A stochastic simulation of 5000 individual molecules, each converting with probability kfdt or kbdt, settles at 0.196 ± 0.006 of them as P and keeps converting in both directions after it has settled.
  • This page. The chamber above integrates the same rate law, counts its own conversions, and the answer letter is chosen at run time by comparing the model's plateau with the plateau of each printed graph — it is not written into the page.
  • Distractor audit. (A) implies K = 1; (B) implies K = 4 — kf and kb swapped; (D) fails the mass balance, has no plateau and bends the wrong way. Only (C) implies K = 1/4.
  • Robustness. The plateau depends only on kb/kf: halving kf and kb together, or starting from any [R]0, changes only how fast the curves settle, never where.
  • Answer key. Checked afterwards against the official final key printed with the paper and against a published worked solution: both give (C), with [R]/[R]0 → 0.8 and [P]/[R]0 → 0.2.

07What we learned

The one idea this question is built on, and where it sits in the syllabus.

KEY CONCEPT

⚖️ Dynamic equilibrium

At equilibrium: forward rate = backward rate.

NOT forward reaction = 0, and NOT backward reaction = 0. Both reactions continue, molecule by molecule. Only the amounts stop changing, because every R that becomes P is matched by a P that becomes R.

kf[R]eq = kb[P]eq  ⇒  [P]eq/[R]0 = kf/(kf + kb) = ?

Live: the molecules keep swapping while the graph line stays flat.

01

The ratio sets where it stops

The plateau depends only on kb/kf. Double both rate constants and it settles in the same place, twice as fast.

[P]eq : [R]eq = kf : kb
02

The sum sets how fast

The distance from equilibrium shrinks as e−(kf+kb)t — faster than kf alone would give, because both directions pull towards the plateau.

kobserved = kf + kb
03

Four checks for any kinetics graph

Starting values right? Sum conserved? Bends the right way? Levels off where the rates balance? A graph that fails any one of them cannot be right.

[R] + [P] = [R]0 at every t
04

The equilibrium constant is a ratio of rate constants

For a one-step reversible reaction, K is the forward rate constant over the backward one — the bridge between kinetics and equilibrium.

K = kf/kb

Formulas used

rf = kf[R],   rb = kb[P]
d[P]/dt = kf[R] − kb[P]
[R] + [P] = [R]0
[P]/[R]0 = kf/(kf + kb)(1 − e−(kf+kb)t)
At equilibrium: kf[R]eq = kb[P]eq,   K = kf/kb
