    Understand the concept

    Count the lone pairs

    The central atom's valence electrons, minus one for each F or Cl bond and two for each O (a double bond), minus the charge, leave the lone-pair electrons.

    LP = (V − e⁻ in bonds − charge) / 2

    Steric number

    Bonded atoms plus lone pairs: the number of electron domains, which fixes the electron geometry.

    SN = atoms + LP

    Lone pairs push harder

    LP–LP > LP–BP > BP–BP. In a trigonal bipyramid lone pairs go equatorial (only two neighbours at 90°); two lone pairs in an octahedron go trans.

    SN 5: LP equatorial

    Shape = atoms only

    The molecular shape is described by the positions of the atoms; lone pairs are invisible in it.

    SN 5, LP 1 → see-saw

    2. Step 2 — Place the lone pairs, name the shapes

      Lone pairs (pink lobes) take space but are not part of the shape. In a trigonal bipyramid they sit equatorial; in an octahedron one lone pair leaves a square pyramid.

      SN 5 with 1 LP (SF₄): the lone pair is equatorial → see-saw. SN 5 with 2 LP (ClF₃, XeF₃⁺): both equatorial → T-shaped. SN 6 with 1 LP (XeOF₄, ClF₅, XeF₅⁺) → square pyramidal. SN 4 with 1 LP (SOCl₂, SO₃²⁻) → trigonal pyramidal, not planar.

    3. Step 3 — Tally and match

      see-saw: SF₄ → 1 → (1)  ·  T-shaped: ClF₃, XeF₃⁺ → 2 → (2)
      trigonal planar: none → 0 → (5)  ·  square pyramidal: XeOF₄, ClF₅, XeF₅⁺ → 3 → (3)

    Independent verification

    • Electron counts. Recounted in a separate script, and cross-checked with the formal-charge Lewis structures (SO₃²⁻: three S–O, one lone pair).
    • Repulsion model. Domains relaxed on a sphere with a 1/r⁶ repulsion, LP weights larger; every lone-pair placement tried — the lowest-energy one reproduces all eight textbook shapes, and the control XeF₄ comes out square planar.
    • Shape from angles. Each shape is named only from the angles between bonded atoms (e.g. see-saw: one ≈180°, one ≈120°, four ≈90°).
    • Solution websites. MathonGo's published solution (their Q46) gives (A).
    • This page. Counts, relaxation, shapes, tally and option are all computed at run time; nothing is written in.
    • Answer key. Checked afterwards against the official final key printed with the paper: Q14 → A.

    04How to use this simulation

    For a student opening it for the first time. The whole investigation runs by itself after one press.

    STEP 1

    Read the mission brief

    Eight species; how many are see-saw, T-shaped, trigonal planar, square pyramidal? Which option matches?

    STEP 2

    Check the values

    The question's species are loaded. You do not need to change anything. The answer is shown above the experiment.

    STEP 3

    Optional: customize

    Swap SO₃²⁻ for SO₃, XeF₃⁺ for XeF₄ or SF₄ for SF₆ and watch the counts change. ↺ RESTORE QUESTION CONDITIONS puts everything back.

    STEP 4

    Start

    Press ▶ START EXPERIMENT under the experiment screen. Nothing else to click (about a minute; SLOW MOTION or 2× changes the pace).

    STEP 5

    Watch the counting and the repulsion

    Each card counts its electrons; then the domains spread over a sphere and the lone pairs (pink) take their places.

    STEP 6

    Shapes, tally, options

    The shapes are named from the bond angles, the species drop into bins and each option is tested. Badges unlock on the way.

    STEP 7

    Explore

    In the VSEPR builder make any species from Xe, Cl or S with F, O and a charge. Then read the Detailed solution.

    STEP 8

    Experiment again

    Press ▶ REPLAY EXPERIMENT, or change a species and press ⚙ RUN WITH CUSTOM VALUES. CLASSROOM MODE enlarges everything for a projector.
