03Detailed solution

Answer: n = — (= √3; the accepted range is 1.70 to 1.75)

  1. Why it stands up. The rod (density ρ) is lighter than both liquids, so buoyancy exceeds its weight. Tilted by θ about the hinge, it is pushed back upright. Take the cross-section as A.
  2. Torques about the hinge for a small tilt θ. Each vertical force F acting a distance d along the rod has lever arm d sin θ. The lower L/2 of the rod sits in the 6ρ liquid and the upper L/2 in the 2ρ liquid.
    buoyancy (lower): 6ρ · A(L/2) · g · (L/4) = (3/4)ρAgL²    buoyancy (upper): 2ρ · A(L/2) · g · (3L/4) = (3/4)ρAgL²
    weight: ρAL · g · (L/2) = (1/2)ρAgL²  ⇒  restoring torque τ = (3/4 + 3/4 − 1/2) ρAgL² sin θ = ρAgL² θ.
  3. Moment of inertia about the hinge: I = (1/3)(ρAL)L² = ρAL³/3.
  4. Period. ω² = τ/(Iθ) = ρAgL²/(ρAL³/3) = 3g/L, so
    T = 2π√(L/3g) = (2π/√3)√(L/g)  ⇒  n = √3 ≈ 1.73.
  5. A detail. As the rod tilts, the interface stays at height L/2, so the length of rod in the dense liquid becomes L/(2 cos θ). That changes the torque only at order θ³. The small-oscillation period is unchanged, and the experiment's 12° nudge shows the tiny amplitude effect.

Understand the concept

For a body pivoted in a fluid, weight and buoyancy both give torques about the pivot, and here buoyancy wins. The rod behaves like an upside-down pendulum held up by the liquid. Each liquid pushes on the part of the rod it surrounds, at that part's centre, so the denser lower liquid acts on a short lever arm and the lighter upper one on a long arm. Here the two buoyancy torques happen to be equal. Their sum minus the weight's torque sets the "spring constant" κ = ρAgL², and the period is 2π√(I/κ). As in the usual textbook treatment, the liquid's drag and the fluid dragged along with the rod (added mass) are neglected.

Key takeaways

  • Buoyancy acts at the centre of the displaced volume, part by part in layered liquids.
  • Torque about a hinge from a vertical force F at distance d along the rod: F d sin θ.
  • Small oscillations: ω² = (net restoring torque per radian)/I. Here ω² = 3g/L and n = √3.
  • I of a rod about one end is ML²/3.
