01The question

Q.7 A quasi-static cycle of a monoatomic ideal gas contains an isothermal process (ab), followed by an isochoric process (bc) and an adiabatic process (ca) as shown in the figure. The volumes of the gas are V1 and V2 at a and b, respectively. If the cycle has heat input Qin and output Qout, then the efficiency of the cycle is defined as η = (Qin − Qout)/Qin. The correct statement(s) is/are:

  3. (C)If V2/V1 = 8, then the temperature of the gas at a is 4 times the temperature of the gas at c.
  4. (D)If V2/V1 = 8, then the pressure of the gas at a is 4 times the pressure of the gas at b.

03Detailed solution

Answer: —

  1. The three states. Take n moles with CV = (3/2)R and γ = 5/3, and let r = V2/V1. Then a = (V1, Ta) and b = (V2, Ta), since ab is isothermal. On the adiabat, TVγ−1 is constant, so Tc = Ta(V1/V2)2/3.
  2. (C) is true. For r = 8: Ta/Tc = 82/3 = 4.
  3. (A) is true. Heat absorbed on ab: Qab = nRTa ln 8 = 3 ln 2 · nRTa ≈ 2.1 nRTa. Heat released on bc: Qbc = nCV(Ta − Tc) = (3/2)(3/4) nRTa = 1.125 nRTa, which is smaller. No heat flows on the adiabat.
    η = 1 − Qbc/Qab = 1 − (3/2)(1 − r−2/3)/ln r ≈ 0.46 for r = 8 (0.464 with ln 2 = 0.7).
  4. (B) is true. Both heats are proportional to Ta, so it cancels in η. The efficiency depends only on r. The experiment runs the cycle at 300 K and at 600 K and measures the same η.
  5. (D) is false. On the isotherm PV is constant, so Pa/Pb = V2/V1 = 8, not 4. The 4 is the temperature ratio Ta/Tc.

Understand the concept

Heat enters only on the isotherm: the gas does work while its temperature, and so its internal energy, stays fixed. It leaves only on the isochore, where the gas cools at constant volume. The adiabat exchanges no heat and simply returns the gas to a. Every heat in the cycle is proportional to the temperature scale, so the efficiency is a pure function of the volume ratio. The ratios are easy to mix up. On the isotherm, pressure scales with 1/V (a factor of 8). Along the adiabat, temperature scales with V−2/3 (a factor of 4).

Key takeaways

  • Isothermal: ΔU = 0, so Q = W = nRT ln(V2/V1), and P ∝ 1/V.
  • Isochoric: W = 0, so Q = nCVΔT.
  • Adiabatic: Q = 0 and TVγ−1 is constant; for a monoatomic gas, T ∝ V−2/3.
  • η = 1 − (3/2)(1 − r−2/3)/ln r, independent of T.
