    Understand the concept

    Rolling without slipping

    The point of the roller touching the fixed disk is momentarily at rest. So the centre moves at v = ωr and the top at 2v.

    vcentre = ω r

    The centre's circle is R + r

    On the outside of the fixed disk the centre stays at distance R + r from O, so its angular speed about O is v/(R + r), not v/R.

    Ω = ω r/(R + r) = ω/51

    The coin-rotation paradox

    A roller taken once round spins (R + r)/r = 51 times, not R/r = 50. The extra turn is the trip round the circle itself.

    spins per lap = 1 + R/r

    Relative angular motion

    Moving in opposite directions, the angle between the centres changes at Ω₁ + Ω₂. The gap must grow from Δθ to 2π − Δθ.

    ωτ = (2π − 2Δθ)/(Ω₁ + Ω₂)·ω

    1. Step 1 — The angle between the centres when they touch

      The two centres are 2r apart, each at distance R + r from O. With sin Δθ = Δθ the chord equals the arc.
      (R + r) Δθ = 2r  ⇒  Δθ = 2r/(R + r) = 2(R/50)/(51R/50) = 2/51 rad

    2. Step 2 — Rolling without slipping sets each centre's speed

      The contact point is at rest, so every point moves as if turning about it: centre v = ωr, top 2v. (Drawn on a straight track; on the rim the centre's path is the circle R + r.)
      v₁ = ω r,   v₂ = 2ω r

    3. Step 3 — Angular speed of each centre about O

      Ω₁ = v₁/(R + r) = ω r/(51 r) = ω/51,    Ω₂ = 2ω r/(51 r) = 2ω/51

      Using R instead of R + r would give ω/50 — the coin-rotation trap. The rollers roll on the outside, so the centre circle is the bigger one.

    4. Step 4 — How far apart they must get

      Start (filled): gap Δθ on one side. Meet (rings): the gap on the far side has closed to Δθ. The angle swept between them is 2π − 2Δθ.
      (Ω₁ + Ω₂) τ = 2π − 2Δθ = 2π − 4/51

    5. Step 5 — The time

      Ω₁ + Ω₂ = ω/51 + 2ω/51 = 3ω/51
      τ = (2π − 4/51) / (3ω/51) = 51 × (2π − 4/51)/3ω
      ωτ = (320.442 − 4)/3 = 105.48  →  option —
      OptionωτWhat it does
      (A) 51(2π − 4/51)/ω316.44uses ω/51 for the relative angular speed — as if only one roller moved

      Traps: using R instead of R + r (50 in place of 51); taking a full 2π; forgetting that both rollers move.

    Key takeaways

    • Rolling without slipping on a fixed surface: the contact point is at rest, so vcentre = ωr.
    • Rolling round the outside of a circle, the centre moves on radius R + r: Ω = ωr/(R + r). A roller spins 1 + R/r times per lap.
    • Two bodies moving round a circle in opposite directions close or open their angle at Ω₁ + Ω₂.
    • "In contact again" for bodies of finite size: the centres must be the contact angle apart on the other side — sweep 2π − 2Δθ, not 2π.
    • Small-angle step: the chord 2r equals the arc (R + r)Δθ when sin Δθ = Δθ; the exact 2 asin(1/51) differs by less than 10⁻⁵ rad here.

    Independent verification

    • Symbolic. sympy derives Ω = ωr/(R + r), Δθ = 2r/(R + r) and τ from the rolling constraint; only option C simplifies to the same expression.
    • Time stepping. Each centre is moved by the no-slip condition alone (the contact point's velocity set to zero), with RK4 and the exact contact angle; the rollers touch again at ωτ = 105.4807, within 0.01 % of C.
    • Coin rotation. The integrated spin is 51 turns per lap, not 50; roller 2 turns twice as far as roller 1.
    • Controls. R/r = 10, 5 and equal or unequal speeds all follow (R + r)/r × (2π − 2Δθ)/(n₁ + n₂).
    • Solution website. MathonGo's published solution (their Q17 = Physics Q.1) gives (C) by the same steps.
    • This page. The bench computes τ from Ω and Δθ and, separately, by stepping the rollers until the centres are 2r apart; the answer is the option that matches.
    • Answer key. Checked against the official key printed with the paper: Q.1 → C.
