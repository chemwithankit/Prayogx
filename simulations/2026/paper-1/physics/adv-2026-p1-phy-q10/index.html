03Detailed solution

Answer: η = — (exactly 1/3; the accepted range is 0.32 to 0.34)

  1. Heat passed down. Each engine turns a fraction η of its input into work and passes (1 − η) of it on:
    Q1 = (1 − η)Q0,   Q2 = (1 − η)²Q0, … ,   Q5 = (1 − η)⁵Q0.
  2. Total work. Energy is conserved across the whole stack, so W = Q0 − Q5:
    ηnet = W/Q0 = 1 − (1 − η)⁵ = 211/243.
  3. Solve. (1 − η)⁵ = 32/243 = (2/3)⁵, so 1 − η = 2/3 and η = 1/3 ≈ 0.33.
  4. The Carnot picture. A Carnot engine has η = 1 − Tcold/Thot, so each step lowers the temperature by the factor 2/3. For example: 1215 K → 810 → 540 → 360 → 240 → 160 K. The whole cascade is then exactly one Carnot engine between 1215 K and 160 K: 1 − 160/1215 = 211/243.

Understand the concept

In a series of engines, the heat each one rejects feeds the next. The efficiencies do not add; the rejected fractions multiply: 1 − ηnet = (1 − η₁)(1 − η₂)…. For Carnot engines this is the statement that temperature ratios multiply, so a chain of reversible engines behaves like one reversible engine between the first and last reservoirs. A common trap is to write ηnet = 5η. That would exceed 1 for η > 0.2.

Key takeaways

  • Engines in series: 1 − ηnet = Π(1 − ηi). For five equal engines, 1 − ηnet = (1 − η)⁵.
  • 243 = 3⁵ and 32 = 2⁵, so spotting the powers gives 1 − η = 2/3.
  • Reversible engines in series are equivalent to one reversible engine between the extreme temperatures.
