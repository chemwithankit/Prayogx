    Understand the concept

    Inductance of a long solenoid

    Inside a long solenoid B = μ₀nI, uniform. The flux linkage is (n l)(B A), so L = μ₀n²A l.

    L = μ₀ N² S d

    Mutual inductance

    The outer coil's field μ₀NI₁ threads each of the inner coil's 2N·(d/2) turns over the inner area S/2.

    M = μ₀ N (2N)(S/2)(d/2) = L/2

    A shorted coil guards its flux

    With no resistance its total EMF must be zero, so its flux linkage cannot change: a current appears that cancels the field inside it.

    L₂ dI₂/dt + M dI₁/dt = 0

    Less field, less inductance

    The field is pushed out of a quarter of the volume, so the energy stored for a given current — and L — drops by a quarter.

    Leff = L − M²/L₂ = 3L/4

    1. Step 1 — Self-inductances

      L₁ = μ₀N²S d = L   ·   L₂ = μ₀(2N)²(S/2)(d/2) = μ₀N²S d = L

      The inner coil has twice the turns per length on half the length — the same N d turns — on half the area.

    2. Step 2 — Mutual inductance

      Flux linkage of the inner coil from I₁: (2N · d/2) turns × μ₀NI₁ × S/2 = (L/2)·I₁.
      M = μ₀ · N · 2N · (S/2) · (d/2) = ½ μ₀N²S d = L/2

    3. Step 3 — The shorted coil

      Zero resistance means zero EMF round the inner coil, so its flux linkage stays at zero: its current makes the field inside it vanish.
      L₂ dI₂/dt + M dI₁/dt = 0  ⇒  I₂ = −(M/L₂) I₁ = −I₁/2

    4. Step 4 — Effective inductance

      V = L₁ dI₁/dt + M dI₂/dt = (L₁ − M²/L₂) dI₁/dt  ⇒  Leff = L − (L/2)²/L = 3L/4

      Energy check: for the same I₁ the field fills 1 − (S/2)(d/2)/(S d) = 3/4 of the volume, so ½LeffI₁² = ¾ · ½LI₁².

    5. Step 5 — Resonant frequency

      The short lowers the inductance, so the resonance moves up by 2/√3.
      ω = 1/√(LeffC) = 1/√(3LC/4) = 2/√(3LC)  →  option —

    Key takeaways

    • Long solenoid: L = μ₀n²A l; for two coaxial coils M = μ₀ n₁ n₂ (common area)(common length).
    • A closed, resistance-free loop keeps its flux linkage fixed: L₂İ₂ + Mİ₁ = 0.
    • A shorted coil coupled to a circuit lowers its inductance: Leff = L₁ − M²/L₂ = L₁(1 − k²).
    • For coaxial solenoids k² is the fraction of the outer coil's volume the inner coil fills — here 1/4 — whatever its turns.
    • Less inductance, higher resonance: ω = 1/√(LeffC).

    Independent verification

    • Symbolic. sympy builds L₁, L₂ and M from μ₀n²Al and μ₀n₁n₂Al, imposes zero EMF on the shorted coil and gets Leff = 3L/4, ω = 2/√(3LC); only option C equals it.
    • Circuit integration. The two coupled coil equations integrated with scipy (no Leff used): the measured period gives ω√(LC) = 1.154701, I₂ = −I₁/2 throughout, energy conserved.
    • Field energy. Flux conservation fixes I₂; Ampère's law gives B = 0 inside the inner coil and μ₀NI₁ elsewhere; the energy integral gives Leff = 3L/4.
    • Controls. Open inner coil: ω√(LC) = 1. Other turns, areas and lengths: always 1/√(1 − a·ℓ).
