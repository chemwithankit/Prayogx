03Detailed solution

Answer: —  P→3, Q→4, R→5, S→2

  1. The condition. The sound splits at S and meets itself at D. The detector hears a maximum when the path difference Δ = (detour length) − l is a whole number of wavelengths. The smallest l > 0 has Δ = λ = 0.29 m. Each detour is a fixed multiple k of l, so l = λ/(k − 1).
  2. (P) A semicircle of diameter l: its length is πl/2, so Δ = (π/2 − 1)l = 0.571l and l = 0.508 m → (3) 0.51 m.
  3. (Q) Up 0.5l, across l, down 0.5l: the detour is 2l, so Δ = l and l = 0.29 m → (4).
  4. (R) Up l, then a semicircle whose diameter is the diagonal l√2. The arc is πl√2/2 = 2.221l, so the detour is 3.221l, Δ = 2.221l and l = 0.131 m → (5) 0.13 m.
  5. (S) The triangle has angles 45° at S, 105° at the top, and so 30° at D. By the sine rule its two sides are l sin 30°/sin 105° and l sin 45°/sin 105°, with sin 105° = cos 15° = 0.97. The detour is (0.5 + 0.707)/0.97 · l = 1.244l, so Δ = 0.244l and l = 1.19 m → (2).
  6. That is P→3, Q→4, R→5, S→2: option (D). Using the exact cos 15° = 0.9659 would give 1.16 m for S, which is still nearest to (2).

Understand the concept

This is interference with a fixed geometry. Both paths start and end at the same points, so only their difference in length matters. Constructive interference needs Δ = mλ. Because every length in each figure scales with l, the difference is a fixed fraction of l. The first maximum therefore needs the shortest l that makes that fraction equal one wavelength. A long detour, as in R, needs only a small l; a detour barely longer than the straight tube, as in S, needs a large one.

Key takeaways

  • Maximum at the detector: Δ = mλ. The smallest non-zero case is Δ = λ.
  • Detour = k × l, so lmin = λ/(k − 1).
  • The detour factors are: semicircle on l, π/2; rectangle, 2; side plus a semicircle on the diagonal, 1 + π/√2; triangle, by the sine rule.
