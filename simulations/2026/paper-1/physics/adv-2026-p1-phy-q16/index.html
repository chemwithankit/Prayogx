03Detailed solution

Answer:  P→5, Q→1, R→4, S→2

  1. The idea. A uniform rod of mass m and length l with one end on the axis, making an angle θ with it: a piece at distance s along the rod is r⊥ = s sin θ from the axis, so I = ∫0l (m/l)(s sin θ)² ds = (ml²/3) sin² θ. A rod parallel to the axis at distance d has every piece at d: I = md². The moments of the rods of a frame add.
  2. (P) Two perpendicular rods, the axis at 45° to each → (5). I = 2 × (ml²/3)(sin² 45°) = 2 × (ml²/3)(½) = ml²/3.
  3. (Q) Equilateral triangle, the axis through C parallel to AB → (1). CA and CB are at 60°: 2 × (ml²/3)(¾) = ml²/2. AB is parallel to the axis at d = l sin 60° = (√3/2)l: m(3l²/4). Total ml²/2 + 3ml²/4 = 5ml²/4.
  4. (R) Square, the axis along the diagonal CA → (4). All four sides make 45° with the diagonal and each has one end on it: 4 × (ml²/3)(½) = 2ml²/3.
  5. (S) Two rods at 30° to the axis → (2). I = 2 × (ml²/3)(sin² 30°) = 2 × (ml²/3)(¼) = ml²/6.
  6. That is P→5, Q→1, R→4, S→2: option (A). Value (3), ml²/12, belongs to a single rod about a perpendicular axis through its centre; no frame here has it.

Understand the concept

The moment of inertia measures how much mass sits far from the axis: each piece counts with the square of its perpendicular distance, r⊥². Tilt a rod towards the axis and its far end comes closer, so its share falls as sin² θ; a rod held parallel to the axis keeps all its mass at one distance. With the same torque, the frame with the larger I spins up more slowly: α = τ/I.

Key takeaways

  • Rod through the axis at angle θ (one end on it): I = (ml²/3) sin² θ; sin 90° gives the familiar ml²/3.
  • Rod parallel to the axis at distance d: I = md².
  • Only the distance perpendicular to the axis counts; moments of the parts of a body add.
  • Same torque, bigger I, slower spin-up: I = τ/α.
