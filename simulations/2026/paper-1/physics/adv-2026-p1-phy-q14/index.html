03Detailed solution

Answer:  P→5, Q→4, R→1, S→3

  1. (P) Aurora borealis → (5). Charged particles from the Sun are guided down the Earth's magnetic field near the poles. They excite oxygen and nitrogen in the upper atmosphere, and the atoms emit light at fixed wavelengths: green 557.7 nm and red 630.0 nm from oxygen, violet 427.8 nm from N₂⁺. A spectrometer sees separate bright lines. Scattered sunlight is a smooth continuum strongest in the violet, so it cannot make a green or red sky.
  2. (Q) Partially polarized sunlight → (4). Air molecules scatter sunlight. A scattering molecule cannot radiate along its own oscillation, so light scattered at angle θ from the Sun has degree of polarization P = sin²θ/(1 + cos²θ): zero looking at the Sun, partial in between, largest at 90°. Total internal reflection reflects both polarizations completely (|rs| = |rp| = 1), so it cannot polarize the light.
  3. (R) Rainbow → (1). Sunlight enters a raindrop, is refracted, reflects once from the back, and refracts out. Because n depends on wavelength (1.331 red, 1.343 violet), the minimum deviation differs by colour: the bow is at 42.4° (red) and 40.6° (violet) from the point opposite the Sun. That is dispersion and reflection. The reflection is not total: the ray meets the back at about 40°, below the critical angle of 48.7°, so most light leaves the drop. Option (2) is the trap.
  4. (S) Dark and bright fringes → (3). Light of one colour passing a narrow opening spreads, and its parts superpose. A single slit of width a is dark where a sin θ = mλ. This is diffraction. Dispersion and reflection cannot make dark fringes in light of a single colour.
  5. That is P→5, Q→4, R→1, S→3: option (A). Option (D) fails on S, (B) on P and Q, and (C) on P, Q and R.

Understand the concept

Each optical effect has one essential mechanism, and a measurement shows which one it is. A line spectrum means atoms emitting. Polarization that grows towards 90° from the Sun means scattering by molecules. A colour-dependent angle with one reflection inside a drop means dispersion and reflection. Dark and bright bands in light of one colour behind an opening mean diffraction.

Key takeaways

  • The aurora is emission (atomic lines), not scattering. The blue sky is scattering.
  • Skylight is partially polarized by molecular scattering, most strongly at 90° from the Sun.
  • A rainbow needs dispersion and one internal reflection. That reflection is partial, not total.
  • Fringes behind a slit or edge are diffraction: dark where a sin θ = mλ.
