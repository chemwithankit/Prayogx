03Detailed solution

Answer: —  v = √(5gR/7) ≈ 0.845 √(gR).

  1. Reaching the edge — no jolt. In rolling without slipping the contact point is momentarily at rest. When that point arrives at the corner, the corner simply becomes the pivot: nothing hits anything, so there is no impulse. The cylinder carries on with ω = v₀/R, now turning about the corner. (Check: angular momentum about the corner before, Icmω + mv₀R = (3/2)mRv₀, equals Icornerω after.)
  2. Turning about the corner. The centre moves on a circle of radius R about the corner. Let θ be the angle of the line corner → centre from the vertical. The speed of the centre is v = Rω, and the moment of inertia about the corner is
    Icorner = ½mR² + mR² = (3/2)mR²,   KE = ½Icornerω² = ¾mv².
  3. Energy. Only gravity does work (the corner does not move). The centre drops by R(1 − cos θ):
    ¾mv² = ¾mv₀² + mgR(1 − cos θ).
  4. The corner's push. Along the radius towards the corner, gravity's component minus the corner's push N provides the centripetal force:
    mg cos θ − N = mv²/R  ⇒  N = mg cos θ − mv²/R.
    As θ grows, cos θ falls and v rises, so N falls. The experiment's N arrow shows exactly this.
  5. Losing contact: N = 0. Then v² = gR cos θ. Put it in the energy equation with v₀² = gR/3:
    ¾gR cos θ = ¾·gR/3 + gR − gR cos θ  ⇒  (7/4) cos θ = 5/4  ⇒  cos θ = 5/7 (θ ≈ 44.4°).
    v² = gR cos θ = 5gR/7  ⇒  v = √(5gR/7) — option (B).
  6. Why it does not leave at once. At the edge (θ = 0) the corner must supply N = mg − mv₀²/R = (2/3)mg > 0. It stays on until θ ≈ 44.4°. In general cos θ = (3v₀²/gR + 4)/7. If v₀² ≥ gR, the cylinder leaves right at the edge with v = v₀: try it with the v₀ slider.

The centre moves on the dashed circle about the corner. N points from the corner to the centre. Friction f acts along the tangent and keeps the cylinder from slipping. The needed amount is f = (1/3)mg sin θ, from the torque about the centre. As N → 0 the ratio f/N grows without limit, so a real corner would let the cylinder slip slightly before θ = 44.4°. The question, and this experiment, take the ideal grip that it states: "rotation around the corner".

Understand the concept

A body turning about a fixed point stays in contact only while the support pushes, N ≥ 0. Contact ends when the pull of gravity towards the pivot (mg cos θ) is no more than the centripetal force the motion needs (mv²/R). Energy decides v; the contact condition decides where. For a rolling body, the kinetic energy about the contact point is ½Icontactω². That includes both the translation and the spin: ¾mv² for a solid cylinder, mv² for a ring, (7/10)mv² for a solid sphere.

Key takeaways

  • At the edge, a rolling body pivots about the corner with no impulse: its contact point is already at rest.
  • Use I about the pivot, (3/2)mR² for a solid cylinder, so KE = ¾mv².
  • Leaving the surface means N = 0, i.e. v² = gR cos θ.
  • Solid cylinder: cos θ = (3v₀²/gR + 4)/7. For v₀² = gR/3 this gives 5/7, so v = √(5gR/7).
  • If v₀² ≥ gR, it leaves at the edge itself.
