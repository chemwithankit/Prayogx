01The question

Q.6 A particle is thrown with a speed v from a point O at an angle θ with the horizontal plane such that it passes through the point P at a height of 1 m and horizontal distance of 5 m from O, as shown in the figure. If acceleration due to gravity is g ms−2, then the correct statement(s) is/are:
  1. (A)If θ = 45°, then v = 5√g/2 ms−1.
  2. (B)If θ = 45°, the particle reaches its maximum height before it reaches P.
  3. (C)If θ = 30°, the particle reaches its maximum height after reaching P.
  4. (D)If θ = tan−1(1/5), then v = 125√g ms−1.

03Detailed solution

Answer: —

  1. The path. With the origin at O:
    y = x tan θ − g x²/(2v² cos²θ).
    Passing through P (5 m, 1 m) fixes the speed for each angle:
    v² = g·25 / [2 cos²θ (5 tan θ − 1)].
  2. (A) is true. At θ = 45°: v² = 25g/[2·½·(5 − 1)] = 25g/4, so v = 5√g/2 m/s.
  3. (B) is true. The highest point is at x = v² sin 2θ/(2g) = (25/4)/2 = 3.125 m, which is before P at 5 m. The ball is already coming down when it passes P.
  4. (C) is false. At θ = 30°: v² = 25g/[2·¾·(5/√3 − 1)] ≈ 8.83g. The highest point is at x = 8.83·(√3/2)/2 ≈ 3.82 m, still before P, not after it.
  5. (D) is false. tan θ = 1/5 is the slope of the straight line OP itself, so 5 tan θ − 1 = 0 and v → ∞. Gravity bends every real path below the line of sight. With v = 125√g the ball passes 0.83 mm below P, and no finite speed reaches it.

Understand the concept

For a given launch angle, only one speed makes the parabola pass through a given point, and it comes from the trajectory equation. The highest point is where the vertical velocity is zero, at x = v² sin 2θ/2g. Comparing that with 5 m tells you whether the ball is still rising at P. Aiming straight at a point (tan θ = height/distance) never works: gravity pulls the ball below the line of sight at any finite speed.

Key takeaways

  • Trajectory: y = x tan θ − g x²/(2v² cos²θ). Put in the point to find the speed.
  • The highest point is at x = v² sin 2θ/2g, half the range on level ground.
  • The highest point here is at x = 12.5 tan θ/(5 tan θ − 1). It lies beyond P only for shallow throws, tan⁻¹(1/5) < θ < tan⁻¹(2/5) (about 11.3° to 21.8°). Try 20° with the slider.
  • A throw aimed along the line of sight to P needs infinite speed.
