01The question

Q.5 Consider a hydrogen atom with vk, rk, and Kk denoting the velocity, orbital radius and kinetic energy of the electron in the kth orbit, respectively. The electron undergoes a transition from the nth orbit, emitting radiation corresponding to the Lyman series. Considering h to be the Planck’s constant and ϵ0 the permittivity of the free space, the correct statement(s) is/are:
  1. (A)Magnitude of change in kinetic energy of electron can be expressed as h/4π · |n vn/rn − v1/r1|.
  2. (B)Magnitude of change in de Broglie wavelength of the electron can be expressed as e²/4ϵ0 · |1/Kn − 1/K1|.
  3. (C)Frequency of the radiation emitted can be expressed as e²/(8πϵ0h) · (1/r1 − 1/rn).
  4. (D)Magnitude of change in total energy of the electron can be expressed as h/2π · |v1/r1 − n vn/rn|.

03Detailed solution

Answer: —

  1. Bohr's rule links everything. In orbit k: m vk rk = k h/2π. The Coulomb force supplies the centripetal force: m vk²/rk = e²/(4πϵ0rk²).
    Kk = ½m vk² = ½(m vk rk)(vk/rk) = (h/4π) · k vk/rk   and   Kk = e²/(8πϵ0rk).
  2. (A) is true. |ΔK| = |Kn − K1| = (h/4π)|n vn/rn − v1/r1|.
  3. (D) is false. The total energy is Ek = −Kk, so |ΔE| = |ΔK| = (h/4π)|…|. The expression in (D) has h/2π and is twice the true change.
  4. (C) is true. The photon carries away hν = En − E1 = K1 − Kn = (e²/8πϵ0)(1/r1 − 1/rn).
    ν = e²/(8πϵ0h) · (1/r1 − 1/rn).
  5. (B) is false. λk = h/(m vk) = 2πrk/k. This is the standing wave the experiment draws: k wavelengths fit around orbit k. With rk = e²/(8πϵ0Kk):
    λk = e²/(4ϵ0 k Kk)  ⇒  |Δλ| = (e²/4ϵ0)|1/(n Kn) − 1/K1|.
    Option (B) drops the 1/n, so it is wrong for every Lyman line (n ≥ 2). For n = 3 it gives 26.6 Å instead of the true 6.65 Å.

Understand the concept

In the Bohr model one rule, m v r = kħ, fixes the whole orbit. The speed falls as 1/k, the radius grows as k², the kinetic energy falls as 1/k², and the de Broglie wavelength grows as k. Exactly k wavelengths wrap around orbit k. The total energy is −K. When the electron drops to orbit 1 it speeds up and its kinetic energy rises, but its total energy falls by the same amount. That energy leaves as a Lyman-series photon in the ultraviolet.

Key takeaways

  • Kk = (h/4π) k vk/rk = e²/(8πϵ0rk), and Ek = −Kk.
  • |ΔE| = |ΔK|: the same expression, not twice it.
  • λk = 2πrk/k = e²/(4ϵ0k Kk). Keep the 1/k.
  • Lyman: hν = K1 − Kn = 13.6(1 − 1/n²) eV, which is ultraviolet.
