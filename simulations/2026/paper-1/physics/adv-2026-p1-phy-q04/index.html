03Detailed solution

Answer: —. The official key accepts both. They are the same lens, with "power" defined two ways.

  1. Each surface refracts by the index difference. A spherical surface of radius R between media n₁ → n₂ has power (n₂ − n₁)/R. For the double convex lens (R₁ = +20 cm, R₂ = −20 cm) in a liquid nL:
    first surface: (1.5 − nL)/(+0.20 m),   second surface: (nL − 1.5)/(−0.20 m),   each = 5(1.5 − nL) D.
  2. Optical power (the surfaces added).
    nL/f = 10(1.5 − nL) D: a straight line from 5 D (nL = 1) through 0 (1.5) to −5 D (2.0) → (B).
  3. Focal length in the liquid (lens-maker's formula). Divide by nL:
    1/f = (1.5/nL − 1)(1/R₁ − 1/R₂) = 10(1.5/nL − 1) D.
    Taking the power as 1/f gives a curve (a hyperbola, bending upwards): 5 D, 0, −2.5 D at nL = 1, 1.5, 2 → (A).
  4. What the experiment showed. At nL = 1.5 there is no index difference, so no refraction. The lens vanishes, f = ∞ and the power is 0 under both definitions. Above 1.5 the difference changes sign, and the double convex lens diverges the light (negative power).
  5. Why (C) and (D) are wrong. They shoot off to ±∞ at nL = 1.5. The focal length does that; the power goes smoothly through zero there.

Understand the concept

A lens bends light only because its index differs from its surroundings. Its power is set by (nglass − nsurroundings). The same double convex lens is converging in air, invisible in a liquid of its own index, and diverging in a denser liquid. "Power" in a medium is written two ways. The optical power nL/f is the sum of the surface powers and is linear in nL. The reciprocal of the focal length, 1/f, is that divided by nL. In air (nL = 1) the two agree. This is why the official key accepts (A) or (B). MathonGo's solution uses 1/f and gives (A).

Key takeaways

  • Lens-maker's formula in a medium: 1/f = (ng/nL − 1)(1/R₁ − 1/R₂).
  • Power is zero, and f infinite, when nL = ng: the lens vanishes.
  • For nL > ng, a convex lens diverges and a concave lens converges.
  • Optical power nL/f is linear in nL; 1/f is a hyperbola. Both fall through zero at nL = ng.
  • A plot that blows up at nL = ng is a focal length, not a power.
