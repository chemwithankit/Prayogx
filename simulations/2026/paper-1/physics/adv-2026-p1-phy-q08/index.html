01The question

Q.8 The electric field associated with an electromagnetic wave travelling in vacuum is given by E0 sin(3y + 4z + ωt)î, where ω is the angular frequency. All quantities are in SI units. The correct statement(s) about this wave is/are:
[Given: speed of light in vacuum c = 3 × 108 ms−1.]

  4. (D)The magnetic field associated with this wave is given by (E0/c) sin(3y + 4z + ωt)(4ĵ − 3k̂).

03Detailed solution

Answer: —

  1. Read the wave vector. The phase is k·r + ωt with k = 3ĵ + 4k̂. So |k| = √(9 + 16) = 5 m⁻¹, and (B), which says 0.5 m⁻¹, is false. The wavelength is 2π/5 ≈ 1.257 m.
  2. Direction of travel. A crest keeps its phase: 3y + 4z + ωt = constant. As t grows, 3y + 4z must fall, so the wave moves against k, along −(3ĵ + 4k̂)/5. (A) is true. (With a "+ωt" phase, the wave travels opposite to k.)
  3. Frequency. In vacuum ω = c|k| = 3 × 10⁸ × 5 = 1.5 × 10⁹ rad/s, so (C) is true.
  4. Magnetic field (Faraday's law). ∂B/∂t = −∇ × E, and ∇ × (Ex î) = (∂Ex/∂z) ĵ − (∂Ex/∂y) k̂ = E₀ cos(…)(4ĵ − 3k̂). Integrating in time:
    B = (E₀/ω) sin(3y + 4z + ωt)(−4ĵ + 3k̂) = (E₀/c) sin(…) · (−4ĵ + 3k̂)/5.
    Option (D) has the opposite sign and is 5 times too large: its |B| would be 5E₀/c, not E₀/c. (D) is false. Check: E × B ∝ î × (−4ĵ + 3k̂) = −(3ĵ + 4k̂), along the direction of travel.

Understand the concept

For a plane wave sin(k·r ± ωt), the wavefronts are the planes k·r = constant. With "−ωt" they move along k; with "+ωt" they move against it. The size |k| = 2π/λ is fixed by the spatial part alone, and in vacuum ω = c|k|. The magnetic field is not a free choice. Faraday's law fixes it: it is perpendicular to both E and the direction of travel, has size E/c, and makes E × B point the way the wave goes.

Key takeaways

  • sin(k·r + ωt) travels along −k̂; sin(k·r − ωt) travels along +k̂.
  • |k| = √(kx² + ky² + kz²), λ = 2π/|k|, and ω = c|k|.
  • B = (1/c) n̂ × E, with |B| = |E|/c. Use a unit vector for n̂, and check that E × B points along n̂.
