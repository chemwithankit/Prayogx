03Detailed solution

Answer:  P→3, Q→2, R→1, S→4

  1. The idea. Only the part of a loop inside x > 0 carries flux: Φ = B · Ain. As the loop turns at ω = 2π/T, a straight edge of a sector sweeps area r²ω/2 per second as it crosses the boundary (the y axis). So the emf −dΦ/dt, and with it i, is a constant while an edge is crossing, and zero while nothing crosses. Positive i is along the drawn arrow (clockwise): it flows while the flux grows (Lenz).
  2. (P) Half-disc → (3). Turning clockwise, its area enters the field steadily for half a turn and leaves steadily for the next half: i is a positive constant, then a negative constant.
  3. (Q) Two 60° sectors on the left → (2). The upper sector enters (0 to T/6), nothing crosses (T/6 to T/3), the lower sector enters (T/3 to T/2); then the upper one leaves (T/2 to 2T/3), a gap, and the lower one leaves (5T/6 to T): two positive pulses, then two negative.
  4. (R) One 60° sector → (1). It enters (a positive pulse, 0 to T/6), stays inside, leaves (a negative pulse, T/2 to 2T/3), then stays out.
  5. (S) Two opposite 60° sectors → (4). One sector is in the field and one is out; they are diametrically opposite, so whenever one crosses out the other crosses in, at the same rate. The wire bends at O without crossing, so both lobes circulate the same way: the total area in the field, and the flux, stay constant, and i = 0. (If the lobes circulated opposite ways, the two changes would add, not cancel, giving pulses that match no graph in List-II.)
  6. That is P→3, Q→2, R→1, S→4: option (C). Graph (5), a current growing in time, would need area entering faster and faster, which a turning sector never does.

Understand the concept

Faraday's law here reduces to how fast loop area crosses the field boundary. A sector edge sweeping across the boundary gives a steady current; a loop wholly inside or outside gives none; area entering and leaving at the same rate cancels. The current tracks dA/dt, not A.

Key takeaways

  • Flux through a loop in a half-field: Φ = B × (area inside the field).
  • A rotating sector's edge sweeps area at r²ω/2: a constant emf while it crosses the boundary.
  • No crossing, no current, even with the loop deep in the field.
  • Lobes of one loop add their flux changes when they circulate the same way.
