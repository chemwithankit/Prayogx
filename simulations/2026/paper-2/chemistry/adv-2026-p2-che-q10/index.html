01Original question — verbatim

Xa+ and Yb+ are hydrogen-like species. The wavelength of light absorbed during the transition between the states with principal quantum numbers n = 1 and n = 2 of Xa+ is λ. The wavelength of light absorbed during the transition between the states with principal quantum numbers n = 2 and n = 4 of Yb+ is 9λ. The lowest possible value of (a + b) is ______.

Section 3 — non-negative integer answer (+4 for the correct value, 0 otherwise, no negative marking). There are no printed options. What there are are four decisions you have to get right on the way, and every one of them is a place students lose the mark. The lab settles all four by measurement.

  • Claim A
    "Hydrogen-like" means the species has exactly one proton.
  • Claim B
    The transition in Y, at 9λ, involves more energy than the transition in X, at λ.
  • Claim C
    λY = 9λX forces ZX : ZY = 3 : 2, so the smallest atomic numbers are 3 and 2.
  • Claim D
    For a hydrogen-like ion the charge on the ion equals its atomic number.

07Step-by-step solution

The solution is still locked

Test all four statements on the bench above and the derivation opens by itself.
Statements tested: 0 of 4 — build an ion, take both spectra, lock the ratio at 9 and count the charges

  1. What "hydrogen-like" actually fixes

    A hydrogen-like species has exactly one electron — the number of protons is free. H, He+, Li2+, Be3+ are all hydrogen-like. That single electron is the whole reason the Bohr result is allowed to run:

    En = −13.6 Z² / n² eV   ·   1/λ = R Z² (1/n1² − 1/n2²)

    It also fixes the bookkeeping you need at the end: one electron and Z protons means the charge is Z − 1, so Z = a + 1. Claims (A) and (D) both die here.

  2. Write down both transitions

    X: n = 1 → 2    1/λ = R ZX² (1/1² − 1/2²) = R ZX² · 3/4
    Y: n = 2 → 4    1/(9λ) = R ZY² (1/2² − 1/4²) = R ZY² · 3/16

    The two geometric factors are 3/4 and 3/16 — the 3's are about to cancel, which is why the numbers come out clean.

  3. Divide, and the constants disappear

    λY/λX = [ZX² · 3/4] ÷ [ZY² · 3/16] = 4 ZX²/ZY² = 9
    ZX²/ZY² = 9/4  ⟹  ZX : ZY = 3 : 2

    R never had to be known, and neither did λ. That is what makes this a ratio question rather than a calculation. Claim (C) is correct.

  4. Smallest integers that fit

    Z must be a whole number, so (ZX, ZY) = (3m, 2m) for m = 1, 2, 3 … The smallest is:

    ZX = 3 → Li  ·  ZY = 2 → He

  5. Atomic number is not the charge

    Each ion keeps one electron, so subtract it:

    a = ZX − 1 = 3 − 1 = 2  ⟹  Xa+ = Li2+
    b = ZY − 1 = 2 − 1 = 1  ⟹  Yb+ = He+

    Stopping at ZX + ZY = 5 is the single commonest way to lose this mark.

  6. Check that m = 1 really is the minimum

    a + b = (3m − 1) + (2m − 1) = 5m − 2

    That increases with m — 3, 8, 13, 18 … — so m = 1 gives the lowest value. Both ions are also genuinely positive there (a = 2, b = 1), which the notation Xa+, Yb+ requires.

  7. Answer

    a + b = 2 + 1 = 3

    Marking: +4 for 3, 0 otherwise. No negative marking, so there is never a reason to leave this blank.

✓ Independent verification

  • Derived before anything was looked up. The ratio, the pair (3, 2) and the value 3 were fixed from the Bohr result alone, and only then compared with a published solution.
  • The geometric factors computed as exact fractions, not decimals: 1 − 1/4 = 3/4 and 1/4 − 1/16 = 3/16, whose quotient is exactly 4. So the condition is 4ZX²/ZY² = 9 with no rounding anywhere.
  • Brute force over every integer pair with 1 ≤ Z ≤ 60: the pairs that give a ratio of exactly 9.000000 are (3,2), (6,4), (9,6), (12,8) … — every one in the ratio 3 : 2, and none outside it. The smallest is (3, 2).
  • Two independent numerical routes agree: Bohr energies with the Rydberg energy 13.6057 eV give λX = 13.5003 nm and λY = 121.5023 nm; the Rydberg constant 1.0973732 × 10⁷ m⁻¹ gives the same two numbers. Their quotient is 9.0000000000.
  • ΔE/Z² checked to be constant across Z = 1, 2, 3, 4, 6 at 2.5511 eV for n = 2 → 4, confirming the Z² scaling the whole argument rests on.
  • The direction of the ratio checked: reversing it to ZX : ZY = 2 : 3 gives 1.7778, and two hydrogens give exactly 4 — neither is 9.
  • The traps reproduced deliberately: ZX + ZY = 5 is a different number from a + b = 3, and a + b = 5m − 2 was confirmed strictly increasing.
  • Answer key cross-check: a published worked solution for this paper reports the same intermediate values — ZX = 3, ZY = 2, a = 2, b = 1 — and the same answer, 3. IIT Roorkee's official key was not retrievable at the time of writing.

Teacher notes

  • Objective. Get students to treat "hydrogen-like" as a statement about electrons, and to finish the question rather than stopping at the atomic numbers.
  • Teaching order that works. Do bench D first. Ask the class what the charge on a hydrogen-like lithium is before any spectroscopy — the wrong answer comes fast and is memorable.
  • Expected wrong answers. 5 by far (ZX + ZY, forgetting the electron); then 2 (using the ratio 3:2 as the answer itself); occasionally 8 (taking m = 2).
  • The step that decides it. 9 = 4ZX²/ZY², not ZX²/ZY². Students who drop the factor of 4 get ZX:ZY = 3:1 and then a + b = 4.
  • Discussion question. "The Y photon has nine times the wavelength. Is it a bigger photon?" Longer wavelength, lower energy — worth the two minutes.
  • Board demonstration. Draw the ladder for Z = 1 and Z = 2 side by side to scale. Identical shape, four times the size. Everything else follows from that picture.
