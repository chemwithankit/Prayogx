02Ozonolysis of natural rubber

Natural rubber is cis-1,4-polyisoprene: the repeat unit –CH2–C(CH3)=CH–CH2– carries exactly one C=C. Reductive ozonolysis (O3 then Zn/H2O) cuts every one of them and caps each cut carbon with =O — the trisubstituted end becomes a ketone, the monosubstituted end an aldehyde. 04Intramolecular aldol — the ring size decides everything

05Why five and not three — measured ring strain

Total strain energies of the cycloalkanes, from heats of combustion. A three-membered ring costs about 27 kcal mol−1; a five-membered ring costs 6. That gap is the whole reason the C5→C1 closure wins, and it is why cyclopentenones are such common aldol products.

06Step-by-step solution

  1. Write natural rubber properly

    Natural rubber is cis-1,4-polyisoprene. Its repeat unit is –CH2–C(CH3)=CH–CH2–, C5H8, with one trisubstituted C=C per unit.

  2. Cleave every double bond reductively

    O3 followed by Zn/H2O breaks each C=C and puts =O on both carbons, stopping at the carbonyl stage (Zn destroys H2O2, so the aldehyde is not oxidised further). The =C(CH3)– end becomes a methyl ketone, the =CH– end an aldehyde.

    ⋯CH2–C(CH3)=CH–CH2⋯  →  CH3–CO–CH2–CH2–CHO  =  X, 4-oxopentanal (levulinaldehyde), C5H8O2

    Each fragment straddles two repeat units, so one repeat unit gives exactly one molecule of X.

  3. Check X against both tests

    CH3CO– present ⟹ iodoform positive  ·  –CHO present ⟹ Tollens positive

    Both tests fit, which confirms X rather than levulinic acid (an oxidative work-up product, no CHO) or pentane-2,4-dione (no CHO).

  4. Let NaOH close the ring — count the atoms

    Number X as C1 (CHO), C2, C3, C4 (C=O), C5 (CH3). An enolate can form at C2 (α to the aldehyde), or at C3 and C5 (α to the ketone). Ring size = |α − carbonyl| + 1:

    C2 → C4: 3-membered  ·  C3 → C1: 3-membered  ·  C5 → C1: 5-membered

    Three-membered rings carry ~27 kcal mol−1 of strain and do not form here; the five-membered closure is both unstrained and kinetically fast. The ketone enolate attacking the more electrophilic aldehyde is also the favoured direction.

    aldol product = 3-hydroxycyclopentan-1-one, C5H8O2 (an isomer of X — nothing is lost yet)

  5. Heat drives the dehydration

    The OH sits β to the ketone, so E1cb elimination of water gives the conjugated enone — the thermodynamic sink that makes aldol condensation irreversible.

    3-hydroxycyclopentanone  −H2O→  cyclopent-2-en-1-one, C5H6O = Y

    Answer: (A).

✓ Independent verification

  • Atom economy: one C5 repeat unit → one C5 product. X is C5H8O2, the aldol is its isomer C5H8O2, and Y is C5H6O = C5H8O2 − H2O. Checked in RDKit: 100.12 − 18.02 = 82.10 = M(Y). No carbon appears or disappears.
  • Both diagnostic tests re-derived from the structure by substructure match (SMARTS [CH3]C(=O)[#6] and [CX3H1](=O)[#6]): X matches both; every other C5H8O2 candidate fails at least one.
  • Ring-size arithmetic done independently of the drawing: enumerating all (α-carbon, carbonyl) pairs on the C1–C5 chain gives ring sizes {3, 3, 5} — only one is viable.
  • Literature precedent: the base-mediated cyclisation of levulinaldehyde to cyclopent-2-enone is a textbook intramolecular aldol; the same 1,4-dicarbonyl → cyclopentenone logic underlies the Hajos–Parrish and Weiss–Cook style closures.
  • Distractor audit: (B) is the 3-membered aldol that ring strain forbids, and it is un-dehydrated; (C) is C10H12O2 — it needs two molecules of X, so it cannot be the major product of an intramolecular reaction; (D) is a C10 bicyclic lactol, again a dimer and requiring a seven-membered closure. Only (A) is C5 and conjugated.
  • Conjugation check: Y must be the thermodynamic product, so it has to contain C=C–C=O. (A) does; (B) does not.
