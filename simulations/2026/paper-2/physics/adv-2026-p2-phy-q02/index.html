Q.2 A nuclear reactor starts producing a radioactive nuclide X from t = 0, at a constant rate of α per second. Each decay of X produces energy E0, which is utilized to heat a liquid of mass m and specific heat s. Assuming no heat loss from the liquid and taking λ as the decay constant of X, the rate of increase in the temperature of the liquid is:
  1. (A)αE0m s(1 − e−λt)
  2. (B)αE0m s(eλt − 1)
  3. (C)λE0m s(1 − e−λt)
  4. (D)E0m s(α − λe−λt)

03Detailed solution

Answer:  dT/dt = (αE0/ms)(1 − e−λt)

  1. Count the nuclei. Let N(t) be the number of nuclei of X present. They are made at the rate α and decay at the rate λN, so dN/dt = α − λN, with N(0) = 0.
  2. Solve it. Separating, dN/(α − λN) = dt, so −(1/λ) ln[(α − λN)/α] = t, which gives N(t) = (α/λ)(1 − e−λt).
  3. Decays per second. The activity is λN = α(1 − e−λt): zero at the start, rising towards α, when the nuclei decay as fast as they are made.
  4. Heat. Every decay gives E0 to the liquid and none is lost, so the heating power is P = E0λN and ms dT/dt = P.
  5. Rate of temperature rise. dT/dt = E0λN/(ms) = (αE0/ms)(1 − e−λt): option (A).
  6. Check the limits. At t = 0 nothing has been made yet, so nothing can decay: dT/dt = 0. After many half-lives the nuclei decay as fast as they are made, α per second, so dT/dt → αE0/(ms). Only (A) does both.
  7. The other options. (B) grows without limit, as if each nucleus made more nuclei; a steady production balanced by decay can never do that. (C) has λ where the production rate α belongs, so it has the right shape but the wrong size. (D) gives E0(α − λ)/(ms) at t = 0, heating before a single nucleus exists.

Understand the concept

A radioactive nuclide that is produced at a steady rate does not pile up for ever. The more nuclei there are, the more decay every second, so the number present climbs and then levels off at α/λ, where decay exactly balances production (the saturation level). The heat released follows the decays, not the production, so the liquid starts warming slowly and approaches a steady rate of rise of αE0/(ms). It reaches about 97 % of that after five half-lives. The same balance explains why a reactor keeps producing "decay heat" after it is switched off: the nuclei already made go on decaying.

Key takeaways

  • Production at rate α with decay constant λ: dN/dt = α − λN and N = (α/λ)(1 − e−λt).
  • Heat follows the activity λN, not the production rate: P = E0λN.
  • Steady state (equilibrium): activity = production rate, N = α/λ.
  • Check every candidate formula at t = 0 and as t → ∞ before trusting it.
