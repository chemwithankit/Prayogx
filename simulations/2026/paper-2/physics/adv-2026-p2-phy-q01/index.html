03Detailed solution

Answer:  vd = 0.208 mm s−1

  1. SI units. A = 0.5 mm2 = 0.5 × 10−6 m2; M = 63.5 g mol−1 = 63.5 × 10−3 kg mol−1; σ = 2 × 108 Ω−1 m−1 (1 mho = 1 S = 1 Ω−1).
  2. Resistance of the wire. R = L/(σA) = 100 / (2 × 108 × 0.5 × 10−6) = 1 Ω.
  3. Current. The wire is in series with the battery's internal resistance: I = ε/(R + r) = 2/(1 + 1) = 1 A. Only IR = 1 V of the 2 V e.m.f. appears across the wire; the other 1 V is used up inside the battery.
  4. Free-electron density. One cubic metre holds ρ/M moles, so n = ρNA/M × (1 electron per atom) = 6.35 × 103 × 6 × 1023 / (63.5 × 10−3) = 6 × 1028 m−3.
  5. Drift velocity. The charge crossing a section per second is I = neAvd, so vd = I/(neA) = 1 / (6 × 1028 × 1.6 × 10−19 × 0.5 × 10−6) = 1/4800 m s−1 = 2.08 × 10−4 m s−1 = 0.208 mm s−1: option (C).
  6. Check through the field. E = Vwire/L = 1/100 = 0.01 V m−1; from J = σE = nevd, vd = σE/(ne) = 2 × 108 × 0.01 / (6 × 1028 × 1.6 × 10−19) = 0.208 mm s−1. The same.
  7. The other options. (A) 0.052, (B) 0.104 and (D) 0.156 are a quarter, a half and three quarters of the true value. Forgetting the internal resistance gives I = 2 A and 0.417 mm s−1, which is not an option at all.

Understand the concept

Switching on a circuit sets up an electric field throughout the wire almost at once, so every free electron starts drifting together and the current appears immediately. The drift itself is extraordinarily slow: about 0.2 mm s−1 here, so one electron would need more than five days to travel the 100 m wire. The current is large because the number of free electrons is huge (6 × 1028 per cubic metre), not because they move fast. Underneath the drift, electrons move randomly at very high speeds, but that random motion averages to zero and carries no current.

Key takeaways

  • R = L/(σA): conductivity is the reciprocal of resistivity.
  • The current depends on the whole circuit: I = ε/(R + r); the wire feels only the terminal voltage IR.
  • Free-electron density n = (ρ/M)NA × (electrons per atom).
  • I = neAvd, equivalently vd = σE/(ne): typically about 10−4 m s−1 in metals.
