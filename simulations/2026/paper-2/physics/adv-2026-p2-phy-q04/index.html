03Detailed solution

Answer:  T = 2πℓ3/(mk2)

  1. Radial motion with ℓ fixed. With the angular momentum conserved, the radial motion is that of a particle in the effective potential Ueff(r) = ℓ2/(2mr2) − k/r: the first term is the centrifugal barrier, the second the attraction.
  2. The circular orbit. It sits at the bottom of the well: dUeff/dr = −ℓ2/(mr3) + k/r2 = 0 gives r0 = ℓ2/(mk), the same as mv2/r = k/r2 with ℓ = mvr.
  3. Stiffness of the well. Ueff″(r0) = 3ℓ2/(mr04) − 2k/r03. Using ℓ2 = mkr0, this is 3k/r03 − 2k/r03 = k/r03.
  4. Small oscillation. A small displacement δr oscillates with ωr2 = Ueff″/m = k/(mr03), so T = 2π√(mr03/k).
  5. In terms of ℓ. With r03 = ℓ6/(m3k3): T = 2π√(ℓ6/(m2k4)) = 2πℓ3/(mk2): option (A).
  6. A check. The orbital period is 2πr0/v = 2πmr02/ℓ = 2πℓ3/(mk2) too. The radial wobble repeats exactly once per revolution, so the nudged orbit closes on itself: it is an ellipse with the centre at a focus, as Kepler's first law says.
  7. The other options. (B) 2π√(m/k) has the wrong units here (k is in N m², so m/k is in s2 m−3); it is the spring formula. (C) and (D) are a third and a fifth of the orbital period; the experiment shows the wobble takes one full revolution, not a third or a fifth of one.

Understand the concept

An orbit can be split into a turning motion and an in-and-out motion. Angular momentum fixes the turning part, and what is left behaves like a ball rolling in a valley, the effective potential. The circular orbit sits at the bottom of the valley; a small nudge makes the ball roll back and forth. For the inverse-square force the valley's stiffness is such that one back-and-forth takes exactly one revolution, so the farthest point comes back at the same place every time and the orbit is a closed ellipse. For any other power law the two periods differ and the farthest point slowly turns around the centre, tracing a rosette: switch the force law in the experiment to see it. Any inverse-square attraction behaves this way: a charged puck around a charged sphere (as in the experiment, where one orbit takes about two and a half seconds) or a planet around the Sun.

Key takeaways

  • Effective potential: Ueff = ℓ2/(2mr2) + U(r); circular orbit at its minimum.
  • Small radial oscillations: ω2 = Ueff″(r0)/m.
  • Inverse-square force: r0 = ℓ2/(mk) and Tradial = Torbit = 2πℓ3/(mk2): the orbit closes.
  • Check the units of every option: 2π√(m/k) is a time only when k is a spring constant.
