Alkyl, Vinyl, Allyl and Aryl Halides: Which Carbon Holds X?

Video not playing? YouTube

Alkyl, vinyl (vinylic), allyl (allylic) and aryl halides differ only in the carbon that holds the halogen. Alkyl: a saturated sp³ carbon (1°, 2°, 3° by the alkyl groups on it). Vinyl: X directly on a carbon of a C=C (sp²). Allyl: X on the sp³ carbon next to a C=C. Aryl: X directly on a carbon of an aromatic ring (sp²), e.g. chlorobenzene. Bonus: X on the sp³ CH₂ next to a benzene ring is a benzyl (benzylic) halide. Identify the carbon attached to X: that's the key.

Key points

  • Three steps: find the carbon bonded to X → is it sp³ or sp²? → what is next door?
  • sp³ C–X: alkyl (R–X; 1°, 2°, 3° by alkyl groups on that carbon), allylic (next to a C=C, e.g. CH₂=CHCH₂Cl) and benzylic (attached to a benzene ring, e.g. C₆H₅CH₂Cl).
  • sp² C–X: vinylic (X on the C=C carbon, e.g. CH₂=CHCl) and aryl (X directly on a ring carbon, e.g. C₆H₅Cl, a haloarene).
  • The trap: a C=C in the molecule does not make it vinylic; X one carbon away from the C=C is allylic. X on the CH₂ outside a ring is benzylic, not aryl.
  • Why it matters: benzyl chloride gives benzyl alcohol with NaOH(aq) easily; chlorobenzene needs 623 K and 300 atm, because its sp² C–Cl bond is shorter and stronger.

The five classes

C holding XClassExample
sp³alkyl (1°, 2°, 3°)CH₃CH₂Cl, (CH₃)₂CHCl, (CH₃)₃CCl
sp³allylic (next to C=C)CH₂=CH–CH₂Cl
sp³benzylic (next to a ring)C₆H₅–CH₂Cl
sp²vinylic (on the C=C)CH₂=CH–Cl
sp²aryl (on the ring)C₆H₅–Cl

JEE Main PYQ

JEE Main 2024 (27 January, Shift 2). Which among the following halide(s) will not show SN1 reaction?

  • (A) H₂C=CH–CH₂Cl
  • (B) CH₃–CH=CH–Cl
  • (C) C₆H₅–CH₂Cl
  • (D) (CH₃)₂CH–Cl

Answer: (B). Classify first. (A) Cl is on the sp³ carbon next to the C=C: allylic. (C) Cl is on the sp³ CH₂ attached to the ring: benzylic. (D) secondary alkyl. (B) Cl sits on a C=C carbon: vinylic, an sp² C–Cl. Losing Cl⁻ would leave the positive charge on an sp² carbon (a vinyl cation), which is very unstable, and the sp² C–Cl bond is also stronger; so (B) does not undergo SN1. Allylic and benzylic cations are stabilised by resonance and the 2° cation by alkyl groups, so (A), (C) and (D) can.

Count the Halogens: Mono, Di, Poly, Gem and VicHalogen compounds are classified first by the number of halogen atoms: one is mono, two is di, three or more is poly (tri-, tetra-). Count the halogens, not the carbons: chloroform, CHCl₃, has one carbon but three chlorines, so it is a polyhalogen compound. Dihalides split by position: both halogens on the same carbon is a gem-dihalide (alkylidene halide, e.g. ethylidene chloride, 1,1-dichloroethane); on adjacent carbons a vic-dihalide (alkylene dihalide, e.g. ethylene dichloride, 1,2-dichloroethane).ChemistryOne Atom, Many Lives: Haloalkanes and HaloarenesIn 1847 James Simpson found that chloroform, CHCl₃, could put people to sleep for surgery. Replace a hydrogen of a hydrocarbon with a halogen and you get a haloalkane (halogen on an sp³ alkyl carbon) or a haloarene (halogen on an sp² ring carbon). They are everywhere: chloramphenicol for typhoid, thyroxine with four iodine atoms, chloroquine for malaria, halothane for anaesthesia. The polar C–X bond, with a δ+ carbon, is why they react, and it opens the whole chapter.Chemistry · 9:16 + 16:9The Polar C–X Bond: Polarity, Bond Length and StrengthThe C–X bond is polar: the halogen pulls the shared pair, so carbon is δ+ and the halogen δ−, and nucleophiles attack that carbon. From F to I the halogen gets bigger, so the C–X bond gets longer (139 → 214 pm) and weaker (452 → 234 kJ mol⁻¹). That is why R–I reacts fastest and R–F slowest, even though fluorine is the most electronegative halogen; and why CH₃Cl, not CH₃F, has the largest dipole moment (μ = q × d).ChemistryHaloalkanes from Alcohols: HX, PCl₃, PCl₅ and SOCl₂One rule: R–OH → R–X, the OH group is replaced by a halogen; only the reagent and its by-products change. HX gives water (anhydrous ZnCl₂ for 1° and 2° alcohols; reactivity 3° > 2° > 1°), PCl₃ gives H₃PO₃ (1 PCl₃ : 3 R–OH), PCl₅ gives POCl₃ + HCl, and SOCl₂ gives SO₂ + HCl, two gases that escape, so the alkyl chloride is pure. Phenol does not react this way: its C–O bond has partial double-bond character.ChemistryFree Radical Mechanism: Halogenation of AlkanesButane + Cl₂ in UV light gives 1-chlorobutane and 2-chlorobutane, because butane has primary and secondary hydrogens. The free-radical chain has three stages: initiation (Cl₂ splits homolytically into 2 Cl•), propagation (Cl• abstracts an H to give HCl and a butyl radical, which takes Cl from Cl₂ and releases a new Cl•) and termination (two radicals combine). The yield of any one product is low: isomers and polychloro products form a mixture that is hard to separate.ChemistryMarkovnikov's Rule: Addition of HX to AlkenesPropene + HI gives 2-iodopropane (major) and 1-iodopropane (minor). In step 1 the π electrons of C=C move to the H of HI, forming a C–H bond on the CH₂ carbon, while the H–I pair goes to iodine (I⁻); the + is left on the middle carbon, a secondary carbocation. A 2° carbocation is more stable than a 1° one, so that pathway is favoured, and I⁻ then bonds to it. Markovnikov's rule: H goes to the double-bond carbon with more H atoms, the halogen to the one with fewer.ChemistryName and Classify Haloalkanes: NCERT Questions SolvedThree NCERT exercise questions solved with one checklist: longest chain (look inside the branches), lowest locants, alphabetical order on a tie; and the class (1°, 2°, 3°) is decided only by the carbon that carries the halogen.
