Alkyl, Vinyl, Allyl and Aryl Halides: Which Carbon Holds X?

Video not playing? YouTube

Alkyl, vinyl (vinylic), allyl (allylic) and aryl halides differ only in the carbon that holds the halogen. Alkyl: a saturated sp³ carbon (1°, 2°, 3° by the alkyl groups on it). Vinyl: X directly on a carbon of a C=C (sp²). Allyl: X on the sp³ carbon next to a C=C. Aryl: X directly on a carbon of an aromatic ring (sp²), e.g. chlorobenzene. Bonus: X on the sp³ CH₂ next to a benzene ring is a benzyl (benzylic) halide. Identify the carbon attached to X: that's the key.

Key points

  • Three steps: find the carbon bonded to X → is it sp³ or sp²? → what is next door?
  • sp³ C–X: alkyl (R–X; 1°, 2°, 3° by alkyl groups on that carbon), allylic (next to a C=C, e.g. CH₂=CHCH₂Cl) and benzylic (attached to a benzene ring, e.g. C₆H₅CH₂Cl).
  • sp² C–X: vinylic (X on the C=C carbon, e.g. CH₂=CHCl) and aryl (X directly on a ring carbon, e.g. C₆H₅Cl, a haloarene).
  • The trap: a C=C in the molecule does not make it vinylic; X one carbon away from the C=C is allylic. X on the CH₂ outside a ring is benzylic, not aryl.
  • Why it matters: benzyl chloride gives benzyl alcohol with NaOH(aq) easily; chlorobenzene needs 623 K and 300 atm, because its sp² C–Cl bond is shorter and stronger.

The five classes

C holding XClassExample
sp³alkyl (1°, 2°, 3°)CH₃CH₂Cl, (CH₃)₂CHCl, (CH₃)₃CCl
sp³allylic (next to C=C)CH₂=CH–CH₂Cl
sp³benzylic (next to a ring)C₆H₅–CH₂Cl
sp²vinylic (on the C=C)CH₂=CH–Cl
sp²aryl (on the ring)C₆H₅–Cl

JEE Main PYQ

JEE Main 2024 (27 January, Shift 2). Which among the following halide(s) will not show SN1 reaction?

  • (A) H₂C=CH–CH₂Cl
  • (B) CH₃–CH=CH–Cl
  • (C) C₆H₅–CH₂Cl
  • (D) (CH₃)₂CH–Cl

Answer: (B). Classify first. (A) Cl is on the sp³ carbon next to the C=C: allylic. (C) Cl is on the sp³ CH₂ attached to the ring: benzylic. (D) secondary alkyl. (B) Cl sits on a C=C carbon: vinylic, an sp² C–Cl. Losing Cl⁻ would leave the positive charge on an sp² carbon (a vinyl cation), which is very unstable, and the sp² C–Cl bond is also stronger; so (B) does not undergo SN1. Allylic and benzylic cations are stabilised by resonance and the 2° cation by alkyl groups, so (A), (C) and (D) can.

Count the Halogens: Mono, Di, Poly, Gem and VicHalogen compounds are classified first by the number of halogen atoms: one is mono, two is di, three or more is poly (tri-, tetra-). Count the halogens, not the carbons: chloroform, CHCl₃, has one carbon but three chlorines, so it is a polyhalogen compound. Dihalides split by position: both halogens on the same carbon is a gem-dihalide (alkylidene halide, e.g. ethylidene chloride, 1,1-dichloroethane); on adjacent carbons a vic-dihalide (alkylene dihalide, e.g. ethylene dichloride, 1,2-dichloroethane).ChemistryOne Atom, Many Lives: Haloalkanes and HaloarenesIn 1847 James Simpson found that chloroform, CHCl₃, could put people to sleep for surgery. Replace a hydrogen of a hydrocarbon with a halogen and you get a haloalkane (halogen on an sp³ alkyl carbon) or a haloarene (halogen on an sp² ring carbon). They are everywhere: chloramphenicol for typhoid, thyroxine with four iodine atoms, chloroquine for malaria, halothane for anaesthesia.
