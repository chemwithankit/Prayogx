Why Metal Feels Colder than Wood

Video not playing? YouTube

A steel railing and a wooden bench at the same 10 °C, yet the steel feels icy. Skin senses how fast heat leaves it, and metal conducts heat hundreds of times faster than wood.

Key points

  • Objects left together reach the same temperature (a thermometer reads both 10 °C).
  • Skin does not sense temperature directly; it senses the rate of heat flow out of (or into) it.
  • Metal is a good conductor: it pulls heat from your finger and spreads it away, so the skin cools fast.
  • Wood is an insulator: heat leaves slowly and the touched spot warms up, so it does not feel cold. On a hot day metal feels hotter for the same reason.

Everyday examples

  • Cooking pans are metal (heat flows in fast); handles are wood or plastic (heat flows slowly).
  • A tiled floor feels colder than a carpet in the same room.
  • A metal seat in the sun can burn, while a wooden bench beside it is only warm.
