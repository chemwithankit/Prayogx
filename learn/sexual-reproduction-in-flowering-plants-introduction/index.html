Key points

  • The flower is the site of sexual reproduction: stamen (male: anther + filament) and pistil (female: stigma, style, ovary).
  • Pollination carries pollen from anther to stigma (wind, water, insects and other animals).
  • A pollen tube grows down the style and carries two male gametes into the ovule.
  • Double fertilisation: gamete + egg → zygote (2n); gamete + 2 polar nuclei → primary endosperm nucleus (3n). Ovule → seed, ovary → fruit.

From pollination to fertilisation

  • Pollination: pollen lands on a compatible stigma (self- or cross-pollination).
  • The pollen grain germinates; the pollen tube grows through the style and enters the ovule.
  • It releases two male gametes into the embryo sac.

Double fertilisation

male gamete + egg → zygote (2n) male gamete + 2 polar nuclei → PEN (3n)

Syngamy and triple fusion together form double fertilisation, characteristic of flowering plants. The zygote grows into the embryo, the primary endosperm nucleus into the endosperm that feeds it.

After fertilisation

  • Ovule → seed; ovary → fruit.
  • Apomixis: some plants form seeds without fertilisation.
