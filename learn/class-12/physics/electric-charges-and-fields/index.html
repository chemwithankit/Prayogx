  3. 3Why a Comb Stays Charged but a Spoon Doesn't: Conductors, Insulators and EarthingRub a plastic comb on dry hair and it lifts paper bits; rub a steel spoon and nothing happens. Rubbing moves electrons, and what happens next depends on the material. Conductors (metals, the human body, the earth) have free electrons, so charge spreads over them and leaks away through the hand and body into the earth. Insulators (plastic, glass, nylon, wood) hold their electrons bound, so charge stays where it was put. Earthing connects a body to the earth, a giant charge reservoir; at home the earth wire and fuse use it for safety, and fuel tankers are earthed to prevent static sparks. Watch
