The Cannizzaro Reaction

Two identical aldehyde molecules, two different products. How an aldehyde with no α-hydrogen, in concentrated alkali, is oxidised and reduced in the same reaction.

Key points

  • Condition: an aldehyde with no α-hydrogen (HCHO, C₆H₅CHO) heated with concentrated NaOH or KOH.
  • Result: one molecule is oxidised to a carboxylate salt, the other is reduced to an alcohol.
  • 2 C₆H₅CHO + NaOH → C₆H₅COONa + C₆H₅CH₂OH
  • Oxidation and reduction of the same compound in one reaction is called disproportionation.
  • The key step is a hydride (H⁻) transfer from one molecule to the other, passed directly, never free in solution.

The mechanism in three steps

  • Step 1, nucleophilic addition: OH⁻ adds to the carbonyl carbon of one molecule, giving a tetrahedral intermediate (O⁻, OH and H on the same carbon).
  • Step 2, hydride transfer: the intermediate re-forms C=O and passes its H, with the bonding pair, directly to the carbonyl carbon of a second molecule. The first becomes an acid, the second an alkoxide.
  • Step 3, proton transfer: the acid gives its proton to the alkoxide: a carboxylate ion and an alcohol.

Why it is oxidation and reduction

CarbonBondsOxidation number
C of C₆H₅CHOC=O, C–H, C–C+1
C of C₆H₅COO⁻C=O, C–O⁻, C–C+3 (oxidised)
C of C₆H₅CH₂OHC–O, 2 C–H, C–C−1 (reduced)

With an α-hydrogen the aldehyde gives aldol instead. Benzoic acid itself is obtained only after the salt is acidified.

Try it yourself

Goal: Predict whether an aldehyde gives aldol, Cannizzaro or neither, and see the oxidation number of the carbonyl carbon change.

  • Choose an aldehyde and count its α-hydrogens.
  • Choose concentrated or dilute NaOH.
  • Predict, then run the reaction.
  • Read the two products and their oxidation numbers.
  • Try HCHO and (CH₃)₃CCHO, then an aldehyde with an α-hydrogen.
