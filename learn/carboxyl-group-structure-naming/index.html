Key points

  • –COOH = a carbonyl (C=O) + a hydroxyl (–OH) on one carbon: carb-oxyl.
  • The carboxyl carbon is sp²: planar, bonds about 120° apart.
  • Resonance: an –OH lone pair moves toward C while the C=O pair moves onto O, so the carbon is less electrophilic than in aldehydes and ketones (no nucleophilic addition).
  • Common names come from the source: formic (formica, ant), acetic (acetum, vinegar), butyric (butyrum, butter).
  • IUPAC: alkane − e + oic acid; the COOH carbon is C-1; two COOH groups keep the e (ethanedioic acid).

Resonance in the carboxyl group

R–C(=O)–OH ⟷ R–C(–O⁻)=O⁺–H

One curved arrow takes a lone pair of the –OH oxygen into the C–O bond; another takes the C=O π pair onto the carbonyl oxygen. The carbon's δ⁺ is partly made up, so it is less electrophilic than the carbonyl carbon of an aldehyde.
