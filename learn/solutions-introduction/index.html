Key points

  • A solution is a homogeneous mixture; the component present in the largest amount is the solvent, the dissolved one the solute.
  • Molarity = moles of solute per litre of solution; also molality, mole fraction, ppm.
  • Henry's law: gas solubility rises with pressure (p = K_H x); opening soda lowers the pressure, so CO₂ bubbles out.
  • Raoult's law: a non-volatile solute lowers the solvent's vapour pressure; colligative properties (boiling point ↑, freezing point ↓, osmotic pressure) depend only on the number of solute particles.

Solution, solvent, solute

Salt water is a solution: water (the larger component) is the solvent, salt is the solute. The mixture is uniform throughout (homogeneous).

Concentration

TermDefinition
Molarity (M)moles of solute / litres of solution
Molality (m)moles of solute / kg of solvent
Mole fraction (x)moles of a component / total moles
ppmparts of solute per million parts of solution

0.5 mol NaCl in 2 L solution → M = 0.5 / 2 = 0.25 M

Henry's and Raoult's laws

Henry: p = K_H · x Raoult (non-volatile solute): (p° − p) / p° = x_solute

Soda is bottled under high CO₂ pressure. When it is opened the pressure falls, the gas's solubility falls, and the extra CO₂ escapes as bubbles. A non-volatile solute takes up part of the surface, so fewer solvent molecules escape and the vapour pressure is lower.

Colligative properties

  • Relative lowering of vapour pressure.
  • Elevation of boiling point.
  • Depression of freezing point (sea water freezes near −1.9 °C).
  • Osmotic pressure.

All four depend on the number of solute particles, not on what they are.
