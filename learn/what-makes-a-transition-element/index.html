Zinc Is in the d-Block, So Why Is It Not a Transition Metal?

Video not playing? YouTube

A transition element has an incompletely filled d subshell in its atom or in a common ion. Iron (3d⁶) passes; copper (3d¹⁰ atom) passes through Cu²⁺ (3d⁹); zinc stays 3d¹⁰ even as Zn²⁺, so Zn, Cd and Hg are d-block but not transition metals.

Key points

  • A transition element has an incompletely filled d subshell in its atom or in any common ion.
  • Fe: 3d⁶ 4s², incomplete → transition metal.
  • Cu: 3d¹⁰ 4s¹ in the atom, but Cu²⁺ is 3d⁹ → transition metal.
  • Zn, Cd, Hg: d¹⁰ in the atom and in their ions → d-block, but not transition metals.

The test, case by case

ElementAtomCommon ionTransition metal?
Iron, Fe[Ar] 3d⁶ 4s²Fe²⁺ 3d⁶, Fe³⁺ 3d⁵Yes (incomplete d)
Copper, Cu[Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s¹Cu²⁺ 3d⁹Yes (through the ion)
Zinc, Zn[Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s²Zn²⁺ 3d¹⁰No (always full)

When a d-block atom forms an ion, the 4s electrons leave first, then 3d electrons.

Group 12

Zinc, cadmium and mercury keep a full (n−1)d¹⁰ subshell in the atom and in their common ions, so they are not regarded as transition metals, though they belong to the d-block.
