One Element, Six Oxidation States: Why Transition Metals Vary

Video not playing? YouTube

The ns and (n−1)d electrons of a transition metal are close in energy, so both can take part in bonding. Manganese (3d⁵ 4s²) shows every state from +2 to +7; the most states occur in the middle of the series, Sc shows only +3 and Zn only +2, and the states change in steps of one.

Key points

  • ns and (n−1)d electrons are close in energy, so both take part in bonding: variable oxidation states.
  • Mn (3d⁵ 4s²) shows +2 to +7: Mn²⁺ (+2), MnO₂ (+4), KMnO₄ (+7).
  • The most states occur in the middle of the series; Sc only +3, Zn only +2.
  • States differ by one (Fe²⁺/Fe³⁺); in the p-block usually by two (Sn²⁺/Sn⁴⁺).

Oxidation states across the 3d series

ScTiVCrMnFeCoNiCuZn
+3+2 to +4+2 to +5+2 to +6+2 to +7+2, +3, +4, +6+2 to +4+2 to +4+1, +2+2

Up to manganese, the highest oxidation state equals the number of 4s + 3d electrons: Sc +3, Ti +4, V +5, Cr +6, Mn +7.

Steps of one, not two

Transition-metal states change one unit at a time (V +2, +3, +4, +5; Fe²⁺/Fe³⁺; Cu⁺/Cu²⁺). In the p-block the common states usually differ by two (Sn²⁺/Sn⁴⁺, Pb²⁺/Pb⁴⁺).
