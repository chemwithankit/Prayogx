Wöhler's Urea and the End of Vital Force

Chemists once believed only living things could make organic compounds. In 1828 Friedrich Wöhler heated an inorganic salt and made urea.

Key points

  • The vital force theory said organic compounds needed a special power found only in living things. Berzelius supported it.
  • In 1828, Friedrich Wöhler heated ammonium cyanate, an inorganic salt, and obtained urea.
  • NH₄CNO → NH₂CONH₂: the same atoms in a new arrangement.
  • Kolbe made acetic acid (1845) and Berthelot made methane (1856).
  • The vital force idea faded gradually, and modern organic chemistry began.
