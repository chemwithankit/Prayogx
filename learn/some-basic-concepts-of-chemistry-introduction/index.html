Key points

  • Matter: solid, liquid, gas; elements, compounds, mixtures.
  • Measurement uses SI units and significant figures.
  • Lavoisier: mass is neither created nor destroyed; such laws led Dalton to atoms.
  • 1 mole = 6.022 × 10²³ particles, a counting unit like a dozen; it powers stoichiometry.

How many molecules in one drop?

A drop of water is about 0.05 g. Its molecules:

0.05 g ÷ 18 g mol⁻¹ = 2.78 × 10⁻³ mol; × 6.022 × 10²³ ≈ 1.7 × 10²¹ molecules

Counting one per second would take about 5 × 10¹³ years, far longer than the age of the universe (about 1.4 × 10¹⁰ years). So chemists count in moles.

The chapter in five steps

StepIdea
MatterStates (solid, liquid, gas) and kinds (elements, compounds, mixtures)
MeasurementSI units, significant figures
LawsConservation of mass (Lavoisier) and other laws of chemical combination → Dalton's atomic theory
Mole6.022 × 10²³ particles; molar mass
StoichiometryAmounts of reactants and products from a balanced equation

Stoichiometry in one line

2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O (2 mol + 1 mol → 2 mol)

So 4 mol of H₂ needs 2 mol of O₂ and gives 4 mol of water.
