Key points

  • Fibonacci sequence: 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 21, …: each term is the sum of the two before it.
  • Ratios of consecutive terms (8/5 = 1.6, 13/8 = 1.625, 21/13 = 1.615 …) approach the golden ratio φ = (1 + √5)/2 ≈ 1.618.
  • Squares with Fibonacci sides tile a near-golden rectangle; quarter arcs through them draw the golden spiral.
  • Sunflower seeds grow about 137.5° (the golden angle) apart, so they pack without gaps; seed spirals often come in consecutive Fibonacci counts such as 34 and 55.

In nature (often, not always)

  • Sunflower heads: 34 and 55 (or 55 and 89) spirals
  • Pinecones: 8 and 13 spirals
  • Many flowers: 3, 5 or 8 petals (lily 3, buttercup 5, cosmos 8)
  • Not every spiral is golden: the nautilus shell's spiral, for example, is not.

Why plants do it

  • Each new seed or leaf grows about 137.5° round from the last (360°/φ²).
  • This angle never lines seeds up in straight rows, so they fill the head without gaps.
  • A simple fraction of a turn, like 135° (3/8), lines seeds into spokes with gaps.
