Losing CO₂: Decarboxylation, Kolbe Electrolysis and Reduction

How the –COOH group itself changes: soda lime removes it as CO₂ (one carbon less), Kolbe electrolysis joins two alkyl groups, and LiAlH₄ or diborane reduces it to a primary alcohol, while NaBH₄ cannot.

Key points

  • Soda lime (NaOH + CaO), heat: RCOONa → R–H + Na₂CO₃, an alkane with one carbon less (CH₃COONa → CH₄).
  • Simple arrow picture: the O⁻ lone pair forms C=O, the C–C pair moves onto the alkyl carbon, CO₂ leaves; the carbanion takes an H.
  • Kolbe electrolysis: at the anode RCOO⁻ → R· + CO₂; 2R· → R–R (CH₃COONa → ethane).
  • LiAlH₄ or B₂H₆, then H₃O⁺: RCOOH → RCH₂OH; NaBH₄ does not reduce –COOH.

Three ways the COOH changes

ReactionReagentProduct (from acetic acid / acetate)
Decarboxylationsoda lime, heatCH₄ (one carbon less)
Kolbe electrolysiselectrolysis of CH₃COONa (aq)C₂H₆ (two CH₃ joined)
ReductionLiAlH₄ or B₂H₆, then H₃O⁺CH₃CH₂OH (1° alcohol)

Kolbe electrolysis at the anode

  • CH₃COO⁻ − e⁻ → CH₃COO·
  • CH₃COO· → CH₃· + CO₂
  • CH₃· + ·CH₃ → CH₃–CH₃
